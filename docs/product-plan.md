# Part 1 — Vision (Fable's opening plan)

**The problem (job-to-be-done):** *"When an AI agent says a feature is done, give me proof — not its word — that a real user could walk through that feature right now, across every service it touches, so I can ship without checking it myself."* Generation became abundant; verification stayed artisanal; and the party reporting success is the one under suspicion. A user-shaped, end-to-end walk across multiple services is the check that would settle it — and it was software's weakest muscle even before AI.

**The ideal experience (7 beats):**
1. State the **promise, not a spec** — one plain sentence, the way you'd tell a colleague.
2. **Hand it over and walk away** — it brings the *whole* system to life the way it really runs; you set up nothing.
3. **Watching is riveting** — it narrates like a QA engineer trying to *break* the change.
4. The verdict is a **judgment, not a checkmark** — works / doesn't / couldn't-honestly-determine-and-why.
5. **Every claim has a receipt** — screen, request, data before/after, timestamp, fingerprint of the exact code that ran; replayable.
6. **Failure is a gift** — separates *your change is wrong* from *the environment was broken*, and hands the agent the repro.
7. **Trust compounds** — "done" starts to mean done; demo anxiety disappears.

**The six verticals:** Intent Capture · Disposable Production · The Skeptical User · Unfakeable Evidence · The Honest Verdict · The Trust Ledger.

**Prototype form factor:** a **CLI** run at the moment of maximum doubt, emitting **one self-contained HTML case file**. The magic moment is **"The Catch"** — feed it a change an agent called done, watch ~90 seconds of a skeptical user, get **DOES NOT WORK** with the broken moment on screen and the receipt attached. Retellable in one sentence: *"It caught my agent lying, and showed me the receipt."*

**The standard to hold:** Playwright's trace viewer (failure explains itself; war on flake), the Rust compiler (error messages as craft — on your side, never vague), SQLite (trust through obsessive, public self-testing — be our own first customer).

**The guardrail against "start but never deliver":** v1 verifies **one flow shape brilliantly**; everything off the straight line from *(one sentence + one running system)* to *(tri-state verdict + replayable case file)* is cut. Weekly ritual: perform The Catch live on a real project — anything that doesn't strengthen that demo is deferred. *"Ship the courtroom, not the legal system."*

# Part 2 — The Build Contract

# Proofbench v1 — The Build Contract

*Synthesis of the six-vertical deep research into the plan we build from. Everything here is behavior — the contract of WHAT the product does. The guardrail governs: everything off the straight line from (one sentence + one running system) to (tri-state verdict + replayable case file) is cut. Exit codes, verdict semantics, and the honesty rules below are day-one public contracts; they freeze now.*

**The one-line product:** a CLI you run at the moment of maximum doubt. One sentence in. ~90 seconds of a skeptical user trying to break the change. One of three verdicts out — WORKS / DOES NOT WORK / COULD NOT DETERMINE — bound to the exact code that ran, delivered as one self-contained HTML case file where every claim has a receipt.

**The three standing quality bars** (applied inline throughout as "Bar:"):

- **Playwright bar** — failure explains itself; capture is invisible, total, and free; war on flake (no sleeps, proven readiness, event-driven waits); the failure artifact is so good people share it instead of describing bugs.
- **rustc bar** — every error message is on the user's side and never vague: what happened, what was found instead, the one command that fixes or reruns it.
- **SQLite bar** — trust through obsessive public self-testing: we are our own first customer; the judge is itself judged, in public, on every release.

# A. Functional Requirements Spec

## A1. Intent Capture — one sentence becomes a checkable promise

**Doctrine (binding):** ambiguity is resolved by grounding + asymmetric defaults + auditability — never by interrogation, never by making the human author anything beyond the sentence. The sentence is half the spec; the running system is the other half. An LLM proposes groundings; evidence disposes. Perfect faithfulness is unreachable; **undetectable** unfaithfulness is what we make impossible.

**Bar (Cucumber's grave):** plain language is for reading, not authoring. The human authors ONE sentence; the product authors the formalization and renders it back as prose. Any design where the human edits the formal artifact is the failure mode.

- **IC-1 (v1)** — One sentence is the entire authoring surface. No template, no keywords, no Given/When/Then, no flags, no schema. A second required input is a regression.
- **IC-2 (v1)** — Every noun and verb is grounded in the RUNNING system before verification starts, each grounding carrying evidence of what was seen and where. Inventing unobserved surfaces is forbidden. **Bar (Testing Library):** grounding vocabulary is strictly user-perceivable, in fixed priority — role + accessible name, label, visible text — never implementation guts.
- **IC-3 (v1)** — "Here's what I'll prove": within seconds, the interpretation is echoed as 5–8 lines of plain prose in the user's vocabulary, then verification starts immediately. Silence is consent; interruption is always available. Never a confirmation gate.
- **IC-4 (v1)** — Every promise compiles to a user-shaped contract: persona + starting state, a walk of only actions a real user could take, success criteria only a user could see, and at least one "it actually took effect" check — the outcome re-observed from a user surface after the action. A promise compiled without an effect check structurally cannot catch "the button exists but nothing happens" — and catching that is the product.
- **IC-5 (v1)** — Every step and criterion is traceable to specific words in the sentence or to a declared assumption. Nothing load-bearing rests on an unstated interpretation.
- **IC-6 (v1)** — Where the sentence is silent, pick the strictest reading a reasonable user would hold, print it in an **Assumed:** ledger (live narration + case file), and name the one-flag override to rerun the other meaning. Never stop to ask. (Asymmetric cost: a lenient default risks false WORKS — product death; a strict default risks a false red that one glance at the echo and one flag fixes.)
- **IC-7 (v1)** — Capture-time tri-state: an uncheckable sentence (subjective terms, unbounded quantifiers, references grounding to nothing) yields COULD NOT DETERMINE, quoting the exact blocking words and offering 1–2 rewrites that WOULD be checkable. **Bar (Hypothesis):** refuse the vacuous green; make "I couldn't really check this" a loud, respected outcome.
- **IC-8 (v1)** — A promised thing no user can find is a CATCH, not an error: "walked everywhere a user can go from the entry point; no way to export exists — here is what IS on the invoices page." The cheapest agent lie ("I added the button" — there is no button) falls out of intent capture itself.
- **IC-9 (v1)** — The case file leads with the sentence verbatim, then the compiled interpretation, then the verdict about that interpretation — including an explicit "what this verdict does NOT cover" line.
- **IC-10 (v1)** — The compiled promise is frozen and fingerprinted into the case file; replay re-runs the exact compiled promise, never a fresh interpretation. Verdict meaning cannot drift between run and replay.
- **IC-11 (v1)** — Failed grounding shows what WAS found: "no Export found; the invoices page has: Download PDF, Print, Share." Never a bare "could not resolve." **Bar: rustc.**
- **IC-12 (defer)** — Interactive one-keypress disambiguation for attended runs.
- **IC-13 (defer)** — Vocabulary memory across runs (requires history — guardrail).
- **IC-14 (defer)** — Multi-sentence promises / ticket ingestion.
- **IC-15 (defer, possibly forever)** — Editable saved-promise artifact. This is Gherkin re-entering through the back door.

**Decisions on open questions (binding for v1):** grounding rubric is: ≥1 evidence-backed grounding per noun → proceed (strict fork if several); 0 groundings + sentence implies the thing is new → verify absence as a catch (DOES NOT WORK); 0 groundings + no novelty implied → COULD NOT DETERMINE. Effect checks are bounded to what pb's own evidence taps can see — user surfaces plus direct read-only datastore inspection; promises whose essence is an unobservable side effect (email delivered, webhook received by a real vendor) answer COULD NOT DETERMINE with the reason. The diff is NOT a v1 input: sentence + running system only. Persona default: the seeded existing user when seeds provide one; brand-new user when the promise implies onboarding and public signup exists; either way, declared in the Assumed ledger.

## A2. Disposable Production — one command conjures the whole system, honestly

**Doctrine (binding):** the honesty boundary is physics of the conjured world — enforced mechanisms plus one verdict rule — never configuration or self-declaration. Classification is computed from what actually served the traffic. The sealed room is default-deny in both directions. A stand-in may convict but never fully acquit.

- **DP-01 (v1)** — One command conjures the WHOLE system from the repo as it sits — every service, datastore, migration, seed — zero environment authoring, zero questions, zero README archaeology. If pb cannot bring the system to life, that is itself a verdict ("couldn't determine: your system never came up, because X"), never a half-started world it pretends to judge.
- **DP-02 (v1)** — The verdict is provably about the exact code the agent claimed done: the case file carries a fingerprint of the code that actually served the walk. Stale builds and cached artifacts are detected and refused — never a verdict on the wrong bytes.
- **DP-03 (v1)** — The conjured instance is production-shaped: services as separate communicating processes, production's own datastore engines, migrations actually applied, the front door reached the way a real user reaches it. Never an in-process harness or single-binary approximation.
- **DP-04 (v1)** — "Up" is a demonstrated fact: each service proves life on its own terms (answered a real request, accepted a real connection, finished its migration); the walk begins only after the front door has served a user-shaped request. No sleeps, no port-open guesses. **Bar (Testcontainers):** wait strategies reject the port-is-open lie; "process started" ≠ "ready to serve."
- **DP-05 (v1)** — Conjuring narrates itself as a launch sequence: each service lights up with its proof of life, so the wait is watchable and mid-launch death shows exactly where and what it said.
- **DP-06 (v1)** — Every case file opens with a **cast list**: each component classified REAL (the code under judgment) / FAITHFUL (production's engine, disposable instance) / STAND-IN (a double for a party we don't run), plus data provenance — computed from what actually ran, never self-declared, never omissible.
- **DP-07 (v1)** — The sealed room: nothing inside touches reality (no email sends, no card charges, no prod datastore reachable); every outbound call routes to a declared double or is blocked-and-recorded as a finding ("your system tried to reach api.twilio.com; nothing was there; here is how it coped"). Kills silently-real and silently-fake in one mechanism. **Bar (MSW):** intercept at the wire, app untouched; undeclared traffic is an error with a name, not a shrug.
- **DP-08 (v1)** — Stand-ins convict but never fully acquit: a crash on a double's well-formed decline is a true DOES NOT WORK; any WORKS whose critical path crossed a stand-in is qualified on the verdict line itself; when the promise's essence IS the third party, the honest verdict is COULD NOT DETERMINE with the path to a real answer named. **Bar (LocalStack):** a double earns trust by enumerating its own behavior and failing loudly at the edge of its knowledge — so v1 ships FEW curated doubles, not a simulated world.
- **DP-09 (v1)** — The walk happens in a world with enough production-shaped data to be user-shaped, sourced from the system's own seeds/fixtures, origin stated in the cast list. Unreachable state → "couldn't determine: no way to reach this state," never an empty-screen false failure, never fabricated state passed off as real.
- **DP-10 (v1)** — Destruction by deadman's switch, not good behavior: verdict, crash, Ctrl-C, or closed lid — everything conjured is destroyed, enforced by a watchdog keyed to the run being alive. Two runs never see each other; a crashed pb never leaves a zombie. **Bar (Ryuk):** cleanup that survives your own death is what earns the right to be run reflexively.
- **DP-11 (v1)** — Environment failure is a first-class verdict with its own receipt: the case file is still produced — COULD NOT DETERMINE with the first causal error, the exact service and its last words, explicitly separated from judgment of the change, phrased like an ally ("this is not evidence against your change"). Never a bare timeout.
- **DP-12 (v1)** — The recipe travels in the receipt: code fingerprint, resolved dependency identities, seed identity, each double's version and scripted behavior, and the one command that re-runs the same judgment against the same world.
- **DP-13 (defer)** — Warm pools / snapshots / instant worlds. The Catch survives an honestly narrated three-minute conjure; it does not survive shipping late.
- **DP-14 (defer)** — Graduation ladder to sanctioned real vendors (drags in secrets + config surface; DP-08's verdict language reserves the slot).
- **DP-15 (defer)** — Learned/recorded doubles. **DP-16 (defer)** — Production-data twins. **DP-17 (defer)** — Many worlds / shared environments. **DP-18 (defer)** — Every system shape; v1 conjures one common multi-service shape brilliantly and declines the rest honestly via the tri-state.

**Decisions:** v1's conjure source of truth is the repo's own compose topology; detected dev/prod divergence (e.g., SQLite locally vs Postgres in prod) is disclosed in the cast list, and the verdict is qualified — never silently absorbed. v1 double roster: outbound HTTP catch-all (block-and-record) plus two curated doubles — payments-shaped and email-shaped — each with enumerated success AND failure behaviors. pb auto-injects typed, obviously-fake credentials (`pb_fake_…`) at boot when apps demand vendor keys, and that injection is itself a cast-list entry. Clock/randomness: record, don't control — honest for v1's replay promise.

## A3. The Skeptical User — the walk that tries to break the change

**Doctrine (binding):** never ask the model "have you tested enough?" or "does this work?" — both invite documented self-preference bias. "Enough" is a closed obligation set created before probing; the stopping rule is mechanical (FALSIFIED / SATURATED / BUDGET — there is no exit labeled "the agent feels confident"); the verdict is computed by deterministic rules over harness receipts; the prober is blind to the builder's diff, commit messages, and claims; the resting state is COULD NOT DETERMINE and only receipts move it.

- **SU-01 (v1)** — Charges before trial: within seconds, the skeptical user announces the 3–7 falsifiable claims it will try to break, and that closed list is the fixed contract for the run. A mis-read promise is catchable in the first five seconds.
- **SU-02 (v1)** — Every probe pre-registers its kill condition — what it tries to disconfirm and what failure would look like. A probe that cannot fail is not admitted to the record.
- **SU-03 (v1)** — Walks as a stranger: reaches the feature through the app's front door, no deep-links, no builder script, human pacing and human mistakes. The stranger's path is where the lies live.
- **SU-04 (v1)** — Cross-examines surfaces against each other: UI claim of success is verified against the API response and the stored data (toast says saved → GET returns it → the row changed). Disagreement between surfaces is itself a falsification — objective evidence no judge can be sweet-talked out of. **This is the catch mechanism for the most common agent lie: surface-deep success.**
- **SU-05 (v1)** — The hostile-first-user repertoire, fixed and visible: wrong/empty/boundary input, double-submit, mid-flow refresh, back-button/out-of-order, re-entry after completion. Inapplicable classes render N/A in the ledger, never silently skipped.
- **SU-06 (v1)** — Verifies the courtroom before the trial: a control probe exercises an adjacent untouched flow first; if the control fails, the verdict is COULD NOT DETERMINE (environment) — never DOES NOT WORK.
- **SU-07 (v1)** — One confirmed catch ends the trial: the first falsification must reproduce on immediate re-run before DOES NOT WORK is rendered (a one-off is "observed once, could not reproduce," recording attached), then the repro is shrunk to the shortest path from app-open to broken moment. **Bar (Hypothesis):** the minimal repro is the true deliverable of failure.
- **SU-08 (v1)** — WORKS is earned, never defaulted: every claim needs affirmative harness-captured evidence AND survival of its mandatory hostile probes; anything less renders COULD NOT DETERMINE naming which claim went untested and why. Absence of failure is never success.
- **SU-09 (v1)** — The agent narrates, the harness testifies: all evidence is captured by the instrumented harness, never asserted by the agent; a narration sentence with no linked harness receipt does not render in the case file.
- **SU-10 (v1)** — Live narration in break-intent language with a claim scoreboard: "Submitting twice fast — real users double-click; expecting exactly one record," while claims flip UNTESTED → SURVIVED / FALSIFIED in real time. The entertainment and the evidence are the same artifact.
- **SU-11 (v1)** — The acquittal is auditable: the case file shows the complete probe ledger — every attack, what it tried to falsify, outcome, receipt link — including the boring survivals. **Bar (Jepsen):** trust compounds through inspectable acquittals, not green checkmarks.
- **SU-12 (v1)** — States residual doubt even on WORKS: one honest paragraph of what a skeptical human should still doubt ("not probed: concurrent users, permissions, mobile viewport"), in the register of "we can prove the presence of bugs, not their absence."
- **SU-13 (v1)** — The judge is itself judged: releases gate on a seeded-lie fixture suite — paired known-good and known-broken-in-a-specific-way builds. A judge that blesses a seeded lie or indicts a seeded truth cannot ship. **Bar: SQLite.**
- **SU-14–SU-18 (defer)** — Adaptive repertoire; multi-user concurrency probing; hostile-attacker persona; catch replay as regression memory; mid-trial human steering.

**Decisions:** blinding the prober is the v1 anti-sycophancy mechanism; different-model-family judging is a post-v1 hardening. Front-door discovery failure within budget is a falsification when the promise implies findability ("a shopper can…"), CND otherwise. Saturation signal: composite novelty fingerprint (new DOM-state hash, new response shape, new console/network error class), K=3 probes without novelty.

## A4. Unfakeable Evidence — receipts by construction

**Doctrine (binding):** the party that benefits from lying must be ARCHITECTURALLY unable to touch the evidence. Recorder beneath (deterministic, sole write handle, observes at layers below the app: browser pixels, pb's own wire tap, direct read-only DB reads, runtime-observed fingerprints), judge after (LLM starts only once the record is sealed; its entire I/O is read receipts + emit prose with citations), renderer between (deterministic; inlines receipts only via hash lookup; judge prose can only point).

- **EV-1 (v1)** — Total capture by default: every screenshot, request/response, DB before/after, console line, and step becomes a receipt automatically — zero flags, zero target-app instrumentation. A run with gaps cannot honestly render a verdict. **Bar (Playwright):** capture must cost nothing to be trusted to exist when needed.
- **EV-2 (v1)** — Code identity is observed, never asserted: the run opens by fingerprinting what is actually running (image digests, commit + dirty-tree hash, served-bundle checksums); the case file headline reads "this verdict applies to exactly <fingerprint>." Claimed-vs-running mismatch is a headline finding.
- **EV-3 (v1)** — Discovery closes before deliberation, visibly: the terminal prints `EVIDENCE SEALED <root fingerprint> (N receipts)` BEFORE the word `JUDGING` appears. The out-of-band terminal seal is an anchor a doctored HTML file cannot retroactively match.
- **EV-4 (v1)** — Every verdict-bearing sentence carries a live citation to its exact receipt. Uncited sentences are typographically flagged UNCITED; a citation that fails to resolve auto-degrades the whole verdict to COULD NOT DETERMINE with the defect shown. Judge hallucination is structurally harmless — it can only point, and pointing at nothing is caught by deterministic code.
- **EV-5 (v1)** — Every receipt declares its capture layer in plain language — "observed at the wire," "read directly from the database," "pixels from the browser," versus "the app's own log line (hearsay)." Hearsay may color narration but can never solely support a verdict-bearing claim. Agent code printing SUCCESS is precisely the lie this catches.
- **EV-6 (v1)** — Before/after data receipts are chain-ordered: "the order did not exist; the button was clicked; the order now exists" is a sequence that cannot be silently reordered or thinned.
- **EV-7 (v1)** — The case file verifies itself, offline: one click recomputes every fingerprint and chain link in the browser — no network, nothing leaves the machine — and flips a banner to INTACT or TAMPERED (naming the broken receipt). **Bar (trace.playwright.dev):** the person the file gets forwarded to is the one who needs the check.
- **EV-8 (v1)** — Every case file carries an honesty panel: "What this seal proves / What it cannot prove" — it proves the judge could not fabricate or alter evidence and the file hasn't changed since sealing; it does not prove the host wasn't compromised or that the recorder saw everything. The stronger tier (signed, externally logged) is named as future. **Bar (SLSA threat tables):** enumerating what you do NOT protect against is what makes people believe the rest.
- **EV-9 (v1)** — The broken moment is a handoff-ready repro receipt: on DOES NOT WORK, the failing step's full bundle (step + inputs, request + response, state before/after, screenshot, fingerprint) is extractable as one machine-readable block an agent can consume, and the case file embeds the whole walk so it can be re-run to mint a fresh, comparable case file. The receipt that caught the lie is the fix's starting point.
- **EV-10 (v1)** — Receipts never leak credentials: obvious secrets are masked at capture time, and each masking is itself receipted (fingerprint of the unmasked original retained) so redaction is visible and can never hide tampering.
- **EV-11 (v1)** — COULD NOT DETERMINE gets the same evidentiary rigor as failure: receipts for WHY — the service that never came up, the fingerprint that couldn't be established. The third verdict reads as a documented finding, never a shrug.
- **EV-12–EV-15 (defer)** — Sigstore-tier signatures + transparency log; rr-grade deterministic replay; cross-run evidence ledger; attested runner (TEE).

**Decisions:** fingerprint floor rule — when code identity cannot be fully established (dirty tree with no hash, unpinned image), WORKS is capped to COULD NOT DETERMINE ("walked it green, but cannot bind the verdict to your code"); this is the single biggest overclaim risk and the rule is published. Judge omission is handled mechanically: the renderer computes an "uncited anomalies" list (failing receipts the judge never cited), and any uncited anomaly degrades the verdict. The wire tap's presence is itself receipted ("all traffic observed at pb's proxy") so first-party network evidence never quietly implies coverage it lacks. Recorder-capture-order is v1's ordering truth; near-simultaneous cross-service effects are phrased as observed-order, not wall-clock truth.

## A5. The Honest Verdict — the tri-state gavel

**Doctrine (binding):** uncertainty is the DEFAULT state of every run; only evidence moves a run out of it. The verdict is a pure function of the sealed evidence ledger, computed by boring deterministic rules. WORKS is the hardest state to reach; DOES NOT WORK has one narrow gate; everything else lands in COULD NOT DETERMINE by construction. It can be wrong; it cannot quietly lie.

- **HV-1 (v1)** — Exactly one of three verdicts. No scores, no percentages, no "mostly passed," no fourth hedge. All ambiguity is forced into the third verdict's reason.
- **HV-2 (v1)** — The verdict block (last thing in the terminal, first thing in the case file) reads in 30 seconds: verdict word, promise restated, one sentence naming the decisive moment ("clicking Pay recorded the full price"), whose side the problem is on, and the single strongest receipt one glance away.
- **HV-3 (v1)** — WORKS is scoped and confessional: exactly what walk, which services, what data — and what was NOT checked. Always "this walk succeeded on this build," never "the feature is correct." **Bar (Jepsen):** underclaiming is why the verdicts get quoted.
- **HV-4 (v1)** — Every DOES NOT WORK is falsifiable: exact broken step in user terms, bound to a specific receipt a skeptical reader can inspect and legitimately disagree with.
- **HV-5 (v1)** — Evidence-completeness gates the pass: a walk that visually succeeded but whose receipt chain has a gap demotes to COULD NOT DETERMINE — "walked it, but can't prove it." Green must be as auditable as red.
- **HV-6 (v1)** — COULD NOT DETERMINE always delivers a fixed contract: (a) what it was trying to establish, (b) the obstacle with its receipt, (c) whose side the obstacle is on, (d) the smallest unblocking action, (e) the exact re-run command. CND the user can convert in one action never becomes the verdict people ignore.
- **HV-7 (v1)** — Blame separation is part of the verdict: DOES NOT WORK = the flow broke inside the system under judgment via the real entry path; environment blockage = CND naming the dependency; pb's own failure = first-person confession ("my fault — pb hit an internal error; your change was not judged") on its own channel that can never impersonate a verdict.
- **HV-8 (v1)** — Nondeterminism is surfaced, never absorbed: a failure candidate triggers exactly 2 identical replays from the same starting state — to measure consistency, never to seek green. All attempts kept side by side; intermittent failure of promise-visible behavior ships as **DOES NOT WORK — intermittent (failed 2 of 3 identical walks)**. A later green never erases an earlier red. **Bar (anti-Bazel):** the honest FLAKY label, without the dishonest counts-as-passed rollup.
- **HV-9 (v1)** — Every verdict is bound to a fingerprint: exact code, environment identity, timestamp, and the case file's own content hash. The verdict is a claim about THIS build at THIS moment.
- **HV-10 (v1)** — Frozen machine contract from day one: exit **0** = WORKS, **1** = DOES NOT WORK, **2** = COULD NOT DETERMINE, **3** = pb internal failure. A crash can never exit 0 and never impersonate a verdict. ("Promise unexecutable as stated" folds into 2, with its own named sub-reason.)
- **HV-11 (v1)** — The verdict is recomputable by the reader: it derives mechanically from recorded evidence; the case file shows which receipts drove it; the narrator's prose can never flip it.
- **HV-12 (v1)** — Time-budget honesty: budget expiring mid-walk → COULD NOT DETERMINE, "ran out of time at step 4 of 7," completed evidence retained, unblocking action = longer budget. Partial progress never rounds in either direction.
- **HV-13 (v1)** — Public seeded ground-truth corpus: known-broken changes, genuinely-done changes, sabotaged environments, planted intermittence — with the confusion matrix published in the repo and re-run on every release. Hard asymmetric budgets: **false-WORKS on known-broken = 0, release-blocking, forever**; known-good → WORKS ≥ 80% (misses land in CND, false-red ≤ 5%); sabotaged-env → CND-with-correct-blame ≥ 90%; overall CND ≤ ~25%, and the only permitted fixes for a high CND rate are hardening the harness — never loosening the WORKS bar. **Bar: SQLite.**
- **HV-14–HV-19 (defer)** — Baseline exoneration run; verdict history/trends; numeric confidence (possibly never); configurable verdict policy; multi-promise aggregation; JSON verdict document for CI.

**Decisions:** subject boundary — the app's own infrastructure edge is subject (a user doesn't care whose 502 it is); the rule is published in every case file. The ~90-second promise applies to the clean-pass path; a catch may honestly take up to ~4 minutes (trial + 2 replays + shrink), narrated throughout. Without HV-14, every DOES NOT WORK carries the standing one-line caveat: "whether this predates your change was not determined."

## A6. The Trust Ledger — the record that outlives the run

**Doctrine (binding):** a verdict never decays as a fact — only as a claim about NOW. v1 stores immutable, self-proving facts; the ledger, freshness, and repair are pure functions computed later over records that already exist. v1's job is to never foreclose them.

- **TL-1 (v1)** — Every run mints one durable verdict record, and the self-contained case file IS that record: promise, verdict, timestamp, fingerprints — readable offline, in full, years later, zero services running.
- **TL-2 (v1)** — Stable citable identity: a short content-derived case ID printed by the CLI and inside the file; the file self-verifies against its ID, so a forwarded case file carries the original's evidentiary weight. **Bar (Rekor):** citability is what turns a log into a ledger.
- **TL-3 (v1)** — Scope of validity in plain words, above the fold: "This is a statement about svc-a@abc123f + svc-b@d4e5f6a as they ran on 2026-07-17 21:40 UTC. It says nothing about any other version."
- **TL-4 (v1)** — The touched-surface manifest: which services, endpoints, and data the walk actually crossed, each fingerprinted — observed, not hand-declared — and narrated ("the walk crossed 3 services and 14 requests"). Skip this in v1 and every old case file is decay-blind forever.
- **TL-5 (v1)** — A machine-readable verdict block embedded in the case file (promise, verdict, subjects-by-digest, manifest, timings) so a future ledger indexes ten thousand files by folding over them — no re-runs, no scraping, no migration.
- **TL-6 (v1)** — A re-opened case file never impersonates the present: verified-at moment and fingerprints are the permanent headline framing. An old green is trustable history, structurally hard to misread as a claim about today. **Bar (Bazel):** memoized trust must be visibly memoized — "(cached) PASSED," never fresh-looking.
- **TL-7–TL-14 (defer)** — Queryable ledger CLI; read-time freshness tri-state (STILL BINDING / STALE / CAN'T TELL); intersection-scoped decay (never TTLs; over-invalidate when forced); smallest-repair suggestions (cargo-vet's move); PR/Slack unfurls; per-promise history; tamper-evident append-only ledger; portable signed attestations.

# B. The Prototype Build Plan — "The Catch"

One flow shape, delivered brilliantly: **a signed-in user performs a state-changing action through a web front door, across at least two services and a real datastore, and the promised effect must actually hold in the data.** CLI in, one self-contained HTML case file out. Everything below is buildable behavior.

## B1. The demo scenario

**The sample app: "Brightcart"** — a deliberately small, deliberately real two-service shop we build and maintain as our own first customer (SQLite bar: it seeds the ground-truth corpus of HV-13/SU-13 from day one).

- `storefront` — a server-rendered web app: product list, cart, checkout page, order-confirmation page.
- `orders` — a separate API service the storefront calls over the network: computes totals, writes orders.
- One real Postgres instance; migrations; seeds (a handful of products, one existing shopper account: `mia@example.com`).
- Brought to life from the repo's own compose topology. Nothing about Brightcart is pb-aware.

**The promise (the one sentence):**

> "A shopper can apply the coupon SAVE20 at checkout and pay 20% less."

**The planted bug (the lie pb must catch):** an agent "implemented coupons" and declared done. The change adds the coupon input, and the checkout page recomputes the *displayed* total in the browser — but the checkout request to the `orders` service never carries the coupon. The confirmation screen says **"$40.00 paid"**; the order row in Postgres says **`total: 50.00`**. Every human demo of this change looks perfect. It is the canonical agent lie: surface-deep success.

Two sibling builds exist from day one: `brightcart@lying` (the bug above) and `brightcart@honest` (coupon actually applied end-to-end). Both are fixtures in the public corpus. Over the weekly ritual (Part C) we add sibling lies: dead route, validation theater, wrong-entity edit, partial rollout.

**The command:**

```
pb "A shopper can apply the coupon SAVE20 at checkout and pay 20% less."
```

Run from the Brightcart repo root. No other input. No flags required, ever.

## B2. The end-to-end behavior, step by step

**1. Compile the promise (IC-1..7).** pb reads the sentence, conjures nothing yet, and within seconds prints the interpretation:

```
Here's what I'll prove:
  As Mia (existing shopper, from seeds), starting at the storefront home page,
  I will add a product to the cart, go to checkout, enter coupon SAVE20,
  complete the purchase — and the amount actually recorded for the order
  must be 20% less than the undiscounted total.
Assumed: "a shopper" = the seeded account mia@example.com  (rerun other reading: --assume new-shopper)
Assumed: "pay 20% less" = the persisted order total, not just the screen  (strictest reading, on purpose)
```

It starts immediately. Silence is consent; Ctrl-C always works. The compiled promise is frozen and fingerprinted (IC-10).

**2. Conjure the world (DP-01..07, DP-10).** The launch sequence narrates: each service builds, migrates, and proves life by answering a real request before the walk begins — no sleeps, no port-open guesses. Fingerprints are read off the live runtime, not off any claim (EV-2). The sealed room is armed: outbound traffic resolves to siblings or is blocked-and-recorded; the deadman's switch is holding the leash. The cast list prints: `storefront: REAL @abc123f · orders: REAL @abc123f · postgres: FAITHFUL (pg16, disposable) · data: repo seeds`. If any of this fails, the run ends here as a fully-receipted COULD NOT DETERMINE (DP-11) — case file still produced.

**3. Charges before trial (SU-01, SU-02).** The skeptical user announces its closed claim list:

```
For this promise to be true:
  C1  a shopper can reach checkout from the front door
  C2  SAVE20 is accepted as a coupon at checkout
  C3  the purchase completes
  C4  the recorded order total is 20% less than the undiscounted total   ← the effect check
  C5  the discount survives the hostile repertoire (double-submit, refresh, garbage coupon)
```

Each probe pre-registers its kill condition. The claim scoreboard starts all-UNTESTED.

**4. Verify the courtroom (SU-06).** Control probe: load the home page, view a product — an untouched adjacent flow. If the control fails, verdict is CND (environment), never a catch.

**5. The trial (SU-03..05, SU-10, EV-1, EV-5..6).** The walk goes through the front door as a stranger — no deep links. Narration is break-intent language while the harness (never the agent) captures everything: pixels from the browser, traffic at pb's wire tap, order rows via direct read-only DB reads, all chained in capture order. Mia signs in, adds the $50 item, enters SAVE20 — the screen shows $40. The cross-examination fires (SU-04): screen says $40 → what did the checkout request carry? → what does the `orders` row say? **The row says 50.00. Surfaces disagree. FALSIFIED.**

**6. Reproduce, then shrink (SU-07, HV-8).** The catch triggers 2 identical replays from a fresh identical world — measuring consistency, never seeking green. 3/3 fail at the same moment: deterministic. Remaining budget shrinks the repro to the shortest path from app-open to broken moment.

**7. Seal, judge, render (EV-3, EV-4, HV-11).** Terminal prints `EVIDENCE SEALED sha256:9f8e… (147 receipts)` — then, and only then, `JUDGING`. The verdict is computed by deterministic rules over the sealed receipts; the LLM writes the narration and can only cite. The renderer inlines receipts by hash, flags any uncited sentence, and degrades on any citation that doesn't resolve.

**8. The verdict block (HV-2) — last thing in the terminal:**

```
DOES NOT WORK                                            case pb-7f3a2c
Promise: a shopper can apply coupon SAVE20 at checkout and pay 20% less.
The screen said $40.00 — the order was recorded at $50.00. The coupon
never reached the orders service. Failed 3 of 3 identical walks at the
same moment. This is a defect in the change, not the environment.
Strongest receipt: confirmation screenshot ($40.00) beside order row (50.00).
Verdict applies to exactly storefront@abc123f + orders@abc123f, 2026-07-17.
Not determined: whether this predates your change.
Case file: ./pb-7f3a2c.html          exit code: 1
```

**9. The case file** — one HTML file, openable offline forever: sentence verbatim → interpretation → verdict block → the broken moment (screenshot + request + data before/after, side by side) → the full probe ledger with the claim scoreboard → cast list and Assumed ledger → touched-surface manifest with fingerprints → machine-readable verdict block → repro bundle an agent can consume → one-click offline self-verification (INTACT/TAMPERED) → the honesty panel ("what this seal proves / cannot prove") → the exact re-run command.

**10. Teardown.** The world is destroyed by the deadman's switch — verdict, crash, or Ctrl-C alike. Nothing survives but the file.

## B3. The acceptance test (the prototype is done when ALL pass)

1. **It catches the lie.** `pb "<promise>"` on `brightcart@lying` → exit 1, DOES NOT WORK, decisive-moment sentence names the price mismatch, the broken-moment receipt shows screen-vs-database side by side, and the verdict block reads in under 30 seconds.
2. **The receipt is replayable.** The embedded walk re-runs from the case file against a fresh conjure of the same fingerprint and mints a fresh case file with the same verdict; the repro bundle, handed to an agent, reproduces the failure.
3. **It does NOT false-green.** The same command on `brightcart@honest` → exit 0, WORKS — scoped and confessional (what was walked, what was not probed) — and it survives the hostile repertoire (double-submit creates exactly one order).
4. **It never rounds up.** Delete one receipt type from capture (fault-injection in pb's own test rig) → the honest build demotes to exit 2: "walked it, but can't prove it."
5. **Blame lands correctly.** Kill Postgres before the run → exit 2, CND naming the dependency, "this is not evidence against your change," case file still produced. Crash pb itself mid-run → exit 3, first-person confession, no zombie processes left behind (verified by the rig).
6. **The seal holds.** Edit one byte of an embedded receipt in the HTML → the one-click self-verify flips to TAMPERED naming the receipt. Terminal-printed seal root matches the file.
7. **A one-off is not a conviction.** Plant a 1-in-3 intermittent variant → verdict is DOES NOT WORK — intermittent with the observed rate, all three attempts shown side by side; a single unreproduced failure reports "observed once, could not reproduce," never DOES NOT WORK, never silently dropped.
8. **Absence is a catch.** Run the coupon promise against Brightcart *without* the coupon feature at all → exit 1: "walked everywhere a shopper can go; no way to enter a coupon exists — here is what IS on the checkout page."
9. **The corpus gates the build.** All of the above run as pb's own release suite; the confusion matrix publishes in the repo; **false-WORKS = 0 is release-blocking.**

## B4. The cut list (deferred, by the guardrail)

No dashboard. No CI/PR bot or JSON verdict doc (exit codes only). No history, ledger queries, freshness computation, or trend awareness (but every case file carries the machine-readable block and touched-surface manifest so the ledger can be built later as a pure fold). No multi-stack matrix — one conjure shape (compose-topology web system); everything else declines honestly. No plugins, no config surface, no auth. No multi-sentence promises or ticket ingestion. No editable promise artifact — ever, until real usage proves the rerun-flag insufficient. No warm pools/instant worlds. No real-vendor graduation ladder, recorded doubles, or production-data twins. No signatures/transparency log/TEE (local tamper-evidence + replay is v1's honest tier, and the honesty panel says so). No adaptive probe repertoire, concurrency personas, security persona, or catch-replay memory. No numeric confidence — possibly forever. No baseline exoneration run (the standing caveat covers it).

## B5. The smallest honest answer for everything v1 cannot verify

The tri-state is the scope valve. Fixed phrasing, always with the receipt, the blocking words quoted, and the smallest unblocking action:

- Unconjurable system shape → *"Couldn't determine: I can't bring this system to life yet — <first causal error, service, last words>. This is not evidence against your change."*
- Uncheckable sentence → *"Couldn't determine: 'feels fast' isn't something I can honestly grade. Two rewrites I could: …"*
- Essence-is-the-third-party → *"Couldn't determine: I can prove your code asked the email service correctly; I cannot prove a real email delivers. Path to a real answer: <named, deferred>."*
- Unreachable state → *"Couldn't determine: no way to reach this state with the seeds this repo provides."*
- Unbindable code identity → *"Couldn't determine: the walk succeeded, but I can't prove which code ran (dirty tree, unpinned image) — so I won't say WORKS."*
- Budget expiry → *"Couldn't determine: ran out of time at step 4 of 7 — everything up to there is receipted; rerun with a longer budget: <command>."*

# C. The Standard & The Weekly Ritual

## C1. Holding the bar — three tests applied to every piece of work

Every feature, message, and artifact must pass its bar before it counts as done:

- **The Playwright test:** *does the failure explain itself, and would someone share this artifact?* Any failure state that requires pb's author to interpret it fails the bar. Any sleep, retry-for-green, or port-open readiness guess fails the bar. The case file is judged by one question: would a stranger forward it instead of describing the bug in words?
- **The rustc test:** *is every message on the user's side?* Each error names what happened, what was found instead (IC-11's "here is what IS on the page"), whose fault it is, and the one command that unblocks. A bare stack trace or "could not resolve" shipping to a user is a release blocker.
- **The SQLite test:** *are we our own first customer, in public?* Brightcart and the seeded-lie corpus live in the repo; the confusion matrix publishes on every release; false-WORKS = 0 is a permanent, non-negotiable release gate. The judge never ships unjudged.

**Standing structural rules** (violations are bugs, not style): the verdict derives only from sealed receipts — no code path lets narration touch judgment; evidence writes exist only in the recorder — no other component holds a write handle; every new claim type must name its receipt type before it may render; every new "can't do X" must land in one of the six honest-CND phrasings, never a bare error.

## C2. The weekly ritual — "Friday, the Catch, live"

The structural defense against "start but never deliver": **the product must perform its magic moment, end to end, every single week, from week one.**

- **The demo is sacred and fixed:** every Friday, run `pb "<promise>"` live against `brightcart@lying`, from a clean clone, on a machine that didn't build it, no rehearsed workarounds, no local state. Whatever exists must complete the full arc — sentence in, verdict + case file out. Week one's arc may be mostly hardcoded scaffolding; it must still be the WHOLE arc. Every week thereafter replaces scaffolding with real behavior without ever breaking the arc.
- **One new lie per week:** each Friday, before the demo, one person plants a NEW seeded lie in Brightcart (from the taxonomy: dead route, validation theater, wrong-entity edit, UI-optimism, partial rollout, planted intermittence) that pb has never seen. If pb misses it, that miss is the week's top priority and enters the corpus permanently. The corpus only grows.
- **The three-verdict check:** every demo shows all three verdicts — the catch (lying build → exit 1), the earned green (honest build → exit 0), and one honest refusal (a sabotaged env or an uncheckable sentence → exit 2). A week where any of the three can't be shown live is a red week.
- **The stopwatch:** clean-pass wall-clock is read out loud every week. It may not exceed the previous week by more than 20% without a named, accepted reason. The ~90-second target is a standing pressure, not a launch-day scramble.
- **The retell test:** after each demo, one attendee who didn't build pb retells the catch in one sentence. If the retell isn't approximately *"it caught the agent lying and showed the receipt,"* the week's polish budget goes to whichever beat broke the story — not to new scope.
- **Scope court:** any proposed work is admitted only by answering one question: *is it on the straight line from (one sentence + one running system) to (tri-state verdict + replayable case file)?* If not, it gets one line in DEFERRED.md and dies for now. The tri-state is the pressure valve: the honest answer to "can pb do X?" is a better CND message, not a new subsystem.
- **Definition of a shipped week:** the Friday demo ran from a clean clone; the confusion matrix re-published with false-WORKS = 0; the new lie is in the corpus (caught, or top of next week's queue); DEFERRED.md absorbed everything cut. Four consecutive shipped weeks with the acceptance test of B3 fully green **is** the v1 launch — there is no separate launch project.

*Ship the courtroom, not the legal system. The verdict never lies, because everything it cannot see, it says it cannot see.*

# Appendix — Per-Vertical Deep Research

*Each vertical researched independently by Fable 5, grounded in successful open-source projects for the quality bar. `v1` = required for The Catch; `defer` = out of v1 per the guardrail.*

## Intent Capture — turn one plain-language sentence into a checkable promise with faithful pass/fail meaning
**Hardest-problem approach.** Doctrine: ambiguity is resolved by GROUNDING + ASYMMETRIC DEFAULTS + AUDITABILITY — never by interrogation, and never by making the human author anything beyond the sentence. (1) The sentence is only half the spec; the running system is the other half. Before any question could even be asked, ground every noun and verb against what a skeptical user can actually reach from the entry point — crawl the user-visible surface, optionally informed by the diff — so 'export' resolves to the one export-shaped control actually found on the invoices page, evidence attached. Most ambiguity that looks hard in the abstract evaporates against a concrete system; runtime NL-against-live-page grounding is proven practice (Stagehand's act/observe), and Testing Library proves user-perceivable semantics (role, label, visible text — in that priority order) are a sufficient and principled grounding vocabulary. An LLM proposes groundings; evidence disposes — no interpretation reaches the runner unless every noun has observed-in-system evidence behind it. (2) Triage the residue into three bins with three fixed behaviors. Harmless forks (any reading exercises the same claim): pick the most user-typical, log it in the Assumed ledger. Material forks (readings would change the verdict): NEVER ask — take the strictest reading a reasonable user would hold, verify that, and print the fork in the case file with a one-flag rerun for the other meaning. The justification is asymmetric cost: a lenient reading risks a false WORKS, the one product-killing outcome; a strict reading risks a false DOES-NOT-WORK that the echoed interpretation exposes in one ten-second glance and one flag fixes. Unresolvable (subjective terms, unbounded quantifiers, zero grounding candidates for something not claimed to be new): COULD NOT DETERMINE at capture, quoting the exact blocking words and offering 1-2 checkable rewrites — the Hypothesis Unsatisfiable stance, refuse the vacuous pass. Special case that pays for the whole vertical: zero grounding candidates for a thing the sentence claims EXISTS is not ambiguity at all — it is the verdict (DOES NOT WORK: no user can find it), receipt included. (3) The human's authoring surface never grows. Their only authoring act is the sentence; their only correction act is choosing a fork the product already articulated (a rerun flag, not a file, not a form, not an edited artifact). The compiled promise is echoed as 5-8 lines of prose and runs immediately — consent by silence — and is fingerprinted so replay can never re-interpret. (4) The honest bound, stated plainly: this approach does NOT read minds and will sometimes misread intent. What it guarantees is AUDITABLE alignment — the product can be wrong about what you meant, but never silently: every reading is visible in one glance, traceable word-by-word to the sentence or the Assumed ledger, strict-by-doctrine when it guessed, and correctable in one flag. Perfect faithfulness is unreachable; UNDETECTABLE unfaithfulness is the thing we make impossible — and that is what keeps the verdict trustworthy.

**Functional requirements:**

- **IC-1** (v1) — One sentence is the entire authoring surface. The user types a single plain-language promise ('a shopper can save an item for later and find it after logging back in') and nothing else is ever required — no template, no keywords, no Given/When/Then, no flags, no schema. Any second required input is a regression. — _The magic moment dies at the first form field. Twenty years of Cucumber adoption data show that the moment humans must author a formal artifact, they stop showing up — the product has lost before it runs._
- **IC-2** (v1) — Every noun and verb in the sentence gets grounded in the RUNNING system before verification starts: 'the invoices page' resolves to a page the product actually reached, 'export' to a control it actually saw, each grounding carrying its evidence (what was seen, where). The interpreter is forbidden from inventing surfaces that were not observed. — _A sentence is ambiguous in isolation but usually not ambiguous relative to a concrete system. Grounding is what makes the promise be about THIS system — hallucinated grounding produces lying verdicts, the one unforgivable failure._
- **IC-3** (v1) — 'Here's what I'll prove' — within seconds, before the walk begins, the interpretation is echoed back as 5-8 lines of plain prose in the user's own vocabulary (who starts where, does what, should see what). It starts verifying immediately; the echo is for glancing, not a confirmation gate. Silence is consent; interruption is always available. — _This is 'show, don't ask' — the only way to make misinterpretation visible in one glance without breaking hand-over-and-walk-away. A wrong reading costs the user ten seconds instead of being discovered after the verdict._
- **IC-4** (v1) — Every promise compiles to a user-shaped contract: a persona with a starting state, a walk (only actions a real user could take), observable success criteria (only things a user could see), and at least one 'it actually took effect' check — the promised outcome re-observed from a user surface after the action (e.g., the saved item is still there after reload/re-login). — _The commonest agent lie is 'the button exists but nothing happens.' A promise compiled without an effect check structurally cannot catch it — and catching it is the product._
- **IC-5** (v1) — Every step and every pass/fail criterion is traceable: it points back either to specific words in the sentence or to a declared assumption. Nothing load-bearing in the verdict rests on an unstated interpretation. — _This is what 'faithful pass/fail meaning' mechanically is — the verdict can be audited against the sentence word by word, so trust in the verdict never requires trust in the interpreter._
- **IC-6** (v1) — Where the sentence is silent (which role? fresh or existing account? which of two similar screens?), the product picks the strictest reading a reasonable user would hold, prints it in an 'Assumed:' ledger in both the live narration and the case file, and names the one-flag override to rerun with the other meaning. It never stops to ask. — _Asymmetric cost: a lenient default risks a false WORKS (product death); a strict default risks a false DOES-NOT-WORK that the echoed interpretation exposes in one glance and one rerun flag fixes. Walking away stays possible because nothing ever blocks on a question._
- **IC-7** (v1) — Capture-time tri-state: if the sentence cannot be made checkable — subjective terms ('feels fast'), unbounded quantifiers with no exercisable instance ('works for every user'), or references that ground to nothing — the answer is COULD NOT DETERMINE, quoting the exact blocking words and offering one or two rewrites that WOULD be checkable. It never proceeds on a promise it can't honestly grade, and never silently narrows one. — _The tri-state is the product's scope valve and honesty guarantee. Hypothesis's Unsatisfiable/health-check stance is the proof this builds trust: refusing a vacuous green is a feature users learn to rely on._
- **IC-8** (v1) — A promised thing that a user cannot find is a CATCH, not an error: if the sentence claims a capability and no user-reachable path to it exists, the verdict is DOES NOT WORK with the receipt ('walked everywhere a user can go from the entry point; no way to export exists — here is what IS on the invoices page'), not a setup failure. — _'I added the export button' (there is no export button) is the cheapest, most common agent lie — this requirement makes the flagship demo of The Catch fall out of intent capture itself._
- **IC-9** (v1) — The case file leads with the original sentence verbatim, then the interpretation it was compiled to, then the verdict about that interpretation — including an explicit 'what this verdict does NOT cover' line (the honest complement of what was checked). — _The reader must be able to audit 'did it check what I meant?' in one glance. Trust compounds only if the verdict never overclaims — a WORKS that quietly covered less than the sentence poisons every future WORKS._
- **IC-10** (v1) — The compiled promise is frozen and fingerprinted into the case file; replaying the case file re-runs that exact compiled promise, never a fresh re-interpretation of the sentence. The meaning of the verdict cannot drift between run and replay. — _Receipts are worthless if the claim they support can silently change. Deterministic replay of a probabilistic interpretation is what makes an LLM-assisted interpreter courtroom-grade._
- **IC-11** (v1) — When grounding fails or wobbles, the message shows what WAS found: nearest candidates in user vocabulary ('no Export found; the invoices page has: Download PDF, Print, Share'). Never a bare 'could not resolve'. — _Rust-compiler / Testing Library standard: errors are on your side, never vague. It also lets the human spot 'oh — the agent named it Download' instantly, which is itself a small catch._
- **IC-12** (defer) — Optional interactive mode: when two materially different readings both have strong grounding, ask exactly one multiple-choice question (A or B, one keypress) instead of taking the strict fork. — _Nice for attended runs, but v1's strict-fork-plus-declared-assumption already covers it without breaking walk-away; adding any question path risks normalizing interrogation._
- **IC-13** (defer) — Vocabulary memory: the product learns the team's nouns across runs ('the dashboard' = the home screen it grounded to last time) so grounding gets faster and more confident over time. — _Compounds trust and speed, but requires history — explicitly out of v1 per the guardrail._
- **IC-14** (defer) — Multi-sentence promises, acceptance-criteria lists, and ticket/PR-body ingestion that yield several promises per run. — _v1 verifies one flow shape brilliantly; batching promises multiplies every ambiguity problem before the single-sentence case has earned trust._
- **IC-15** (defer) — An editable saved-promise artifact humans can hand-tune between runs. — _This is the Gherkin trap re-entering through the back door — the moment humans maintain the formal artifact, the product has lost. Revisit only if real usage proves the one-flag fork override insufficient._

**Learn from (quality bar):**

- **Cucumber / Gherkin** (https://cucumber.io/blog/collaboration/the-worlds-most-misunderstood-collaboration-tool/) — The canonical cautionary tale for exactly this vertical: plain language is for READING, not authoring. Cucumber's creator calls it 'the world's most misunderstood collaboration tool' — teams that treated Gherkin as a test-authoring syntax watched business people never show up, and scenarios written after the code became brittle and lost their documentation value. The steal is the inversion: the human authors ONE sentence; the PRODUCT authors and maintains the formalization, then renders it back as readable prose — keeping Gherkin's legibility (a promise anyone can read) while charging the human zero authorship tax. Any design where the human edits the formal artifact is the failure mode, not a feature.
- **Testing Library** (https://testing-library.com/docs/guiding-principles/) — Disambiguation by constrained vocabulary. Its whole trust story is one principle — 'the more your tests resemble the way your software is used, the more confidence they can give you' — enforced by a strict query priority: things any user can perceive first (role + accessible name, label, visible text), invisible test-ids last because 'the user cannot see these.' That priority order IS an ambiguity-resolution policy: when a promise says 'the export button,' there is a principled, fixed order for what that can mean, all of it user-perceivable, none of it implementation guts. And when a query fails, it throws a descriptive error showing what was actually available — the model for IC-11's 'here's what I did find.' Faithful pass/fail meaning comes from refusing any vocabulary a real user couldn't perceive.
- **Hypothesis** (https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/) — Institutionalized refusal to fake a pass, plus machine-authored formalization with honest uncertainty. Health checks (filter_too_much, too_slow) and the Unsatisfiable error make the tool police whether its own check was MEANINGFUL — it raises rather than reporting a vacuous green, and users love it for that. Shrinking reports every failure as the minimal counterexample, in the user's terms. And the Ghostwriter ('hypothesis write') is the proof-of-concept for this vertical's core move: the tool writes the formal test FOR you from a signature, using property templates where it detects them (roundtrip, idempotence), and where it cannot infer a property it emits explicit 'TODO: replace st.nothing()' markers and weak fuzz tests instead of pretending it produced a spec. Steal both: capture-time refusal with the blocking words quoted, and generated formalization that marks its own uncertainty instead of burying it.

**Open questions:** The fork-vs-unresolvable rubric needs a hard line. Proposal: >=1 evidence-backed grounding per noun => proceed (strict fork if several); 0 groundings + sentence implies the thing is new => verify absence as a catch (DOES NOT WORK); 0 groundings + no novelty implied => COULD NOT DETERMINE. Does this rubric survive promises about non-visible behavior ('a confirmation email is sent') where a user-shaped observer can't see the effect?; Effect checks for promises whose outcome isn't user-visible (email sent, webhook fired, background job): does v1 bound itself to user-visible effects only and answer COULD NOT DETERMINE for the rest (cleanest), or allow exactly one privileged observation point (e.g., a mail catcher) at the cost of setup surface?; Is the diff a v1 input? It sharpens grounding (which of two Export buttons is NEW), enables the code fingerprint receipt, and helps the novelty-implied rubric — but adds integration surface. Sentence + running system may be enough for The Catch; decide before the interpreter's contract freezes.; Persona default when the sentence is silent: 'brand-new user' is the strictest and catches onboarding-path lies, but may be un-exercisable without signup/seed data; 'existing typical user' is exercisable but lenient. Which default lies less often, and should the choice depend on what the entry point offers (public signup present => brand-new)?; Negative and regression promises ('users can NO LONGER see other tenants' invoices', 'the crash on empty cart is fixed') — same single flow shape or out of v1? Absence-proofs have a different honesty profile (can't walk everywhere), so they may need their own COULD-NOT-DETERMINE wording even if accepted.; How strict is too strict before the doctrine reads as pedantry? Should the case file name the policy explicitly ('read strictly, on purpose — lenient rerun: --meaning B') to convert annoyance into trust, and is one sentence of doctrine in every case file worth the space?; Where does learned vocabulary live post-v1 (team nouns -> grounded surfaces) so grounding compounds without becoming a config surface or a history ledger the guardrail forbids?


## Disposable Production
**Hardest-problem approach.** Treat the honesty boundary as a physics of the conjured world — enforced mechanisms plus one verdict rule — never as configuration or self-declaration. (1) CLASSIFY BY WHO WROTE THE RUNNING CODE, COMPUTED AT THE WIRE. Every component gets a reality class derived from observed facts (what artifact actually served the bytes), not from a manifest anyone typed: REAL = built from the judged fingerprint (the change itself); FAITHFUL = production's own engine in a disposable instance (real Postgres/Redis/Kafka — trust the engine, own the instance); STAND-IN = anything answering for a party we don't run (payments, email, SMS, LLM/partner APIs — including protocol-compatible reimplementations, which are doubles no matter how good). Data is classified the same way (repo seeds / synthetic / empty). Because the classifier watches what served the traffic, a stand-in cannot be misfiled as real. (2) THE SEALED ROOM: DEFAULT-DENY IN BOTH DIRECTIONS. The world runs inside a boundary where every outbound connection resolves to a sibling conjured service, or to one of a small set of curated, versioned doubles pb ships for the top boundaries (payments, email, object storage, outbound webhooks) with realistic success AND failure behaviors — or it is BLOCKED and recorded (destination, calling service, timestamp, how the app coped) as a finding in the case file. Real side effects are physically impossible (the email cannot send, the card cannot charge, prod data is unreachable), and an undeclared destination can never quietly hand back a 200. This kills both dishonesty modes at once: silently-real and silently-fake. (3) TAINT THE EVIDENCE, NOT JUST THE CAST LIST. Every receipt carries the reality class of every boundary its evidence crossed — the 'Payment successful' screenshot is stamped 'payments: STAND-IN (double v1.3, canned approval)' — and taint propagates down the walk to everything built on that response. The case file renders tainted evidence visibly distinct: it is impossible to read the file and mistake fake for real. (4) THE VERDICT ASYMMETRY — the rule that keeps pb honest forever: a stand-in may CONVICT but never fully ACQUIT. An app that crashes on the double's well-formed decline is truly broken (the double just delivered the mail), so DOES NOT WORK through a stand-in stands at full strength. But WORKS is qualified on the verdict line itself whenever the critical path crossed a double — naming what was proven (well-formed requests, responses handled) and what was not (that the real vendor would accept them) — and when the promise's essence IS the third party ('emails actually deliver'), the answer is COULDN'T HONESTLY DETERMINE, with the reason and the named path to a real answer (the deferred graduation ladder: bring a sandbox key, rerun, upgrade the verdict). Honestly BOUNDED, not fake-solved: pb does not claim to simulate third parties faithfully — LocalStack demonstrates that faithful emulation is a company-sized parity-accounting effort — so v1 ships few curated doubles whose exact behaviors are enumerated in every case file, blocks-and-reports everything else, and lets the tri-state absorb what the sealed room cannot prove. The product never lies because it only ever claims what the sealed room lets it demonstrate."

**Functional requirements:**

- **DP-01** (v1) — One command conjures the WHOLE system from the repo as it sits — every service, its datastores, migrations, and seeds come to life with zero environment authoring, zero questions, zero README archaeology. If pb cannot bring the system to life, that is itself a verdict ('couldn't determine: your system never came up, because X'), never a half-started world it pretends to judge. — _Beat 2 (hand over and walk away) is the precondition for The Catch; at the moment of maximum doubt the user has zero patience for setup, and a partially-alive system silently judged is the first way to lose them forever._
- **DP-02** (v1) — The verdict is provably about the exact code the agent claimed was done: the case file carries a fingerprint of the code that actually served the walk, verified to match the change under judgment. A stale build or cached artifact is detected and refused — pb never delivers a verdict on the wrong bytes. — _Beat 5's fingerprint receipt is the anti-faking core: 'it caught my agent lying' only lands if nobody can argue the wrong code ran. A confident verdict about the wrong code is worse than no verdict._
- **DP-03** (v1) — The conjured instance is production-shaped, not test-shaped: services run as separate communicating processes, datastores are the same engines production uses, migrations are actually applied, and the front door is reached the way a real user reaches it — never an in-process harness or single-binary approximation of the system. — _The JTBD is a user-shaped walk 'across every service it touches.' If the SYSTEM itself is a stand-in, every receipt downstream is inflated — this is the biggest possible violation of the honesty boundary and it happens by default in most test tooling._
- **DP-04** (v1) — 'The system is up' is a demonstrated fact, never an assumption: each service proves life on its own terms (answered a real request, accepted a real connection, finished its migration), and the walk begins only after the front door has served a user-shaped request. No sleeps, no port-open guesses standing in for readiness. — _The war on flake starts here — most 'the test is flaky' distrust is actually 'we started judging before the world was ready.' Proven readiness is also what makes DP-11's env-vs-change separation honest instead of a coin flip._
- **DP-05** (v1) — Conjuring narrates itself like a launch sequence: the user watches each service light up with its proof of life (built, migrated, answered first request), so the wait is watchable and when something dies mid-launch you saw exactly where and what it said. — _Beat 3 (watching is riveting) must not begin at the walk — conjure time is otherwise dead air that kills the ~90s demo, and the launch narration doubles as the receipt trail when the environment is what failed._
- **DP-06** (v1) — Every case file opens with a cast list: each component of the conjured world classified as REAL (the code under judgment), FAITHFUL (production's own engine, disposable instance), or STAND-IN (a double playing a party we don't run — payments, email, third-party APIs), plus the provenance of every byte of data. The classification is computed from what actually ran and served, never self-declared, never omissible. — _This is the hardest problem made permanently visible: nobody can finish reading a case file without knowing exactly what was real. An honesty disclosure that is declared rather than computed would itself be a lie waiting to happen._
- **DP-07** (v1) — The world is a sealed room: nothing inside it can touch reality (no email actually sends, no card is actually charged, no production datastore is reachable), and nothing crosses the boundary silently in either direction — every outbound call is routed to a declared double or blocked-and-recorded, and an attempt to reach an undeclared destination appears in the case file as a finding ('your system tried to reach api.twilio.com; nothing was there; here is how it coped'). — _Kills both dishonesty failure modes at once: silently-real (a 2am verification run that sends real emails is a catastrophe) and silently-fake (an unnoticed canned 200 inflating a WORKS). Safety and verdict integrity are the same mechanism._
- **DP-08** (v1) — Stand-ins may convict but never fully acquit: a failure through a double is a true DOES NOT WORK (the app crashing on a well-formed decline is the app's bug), but any WORKS whose critical path crossed a stand-in is qualified on the verdict line itself — naming the double, what was verified (your code sent well-formed requests and handled the responses) and what was not (that the real vendor would accept them). When the promise's essence IS the third party ('emails actually deliver'), the honest verdict is COULDN'T DETERMINE, with the reason and the path to a real answer. — _This asymmetry is the entire honesty boundary compressed into one rule, and it is what keeps 'WORKS' meaning works. The tri-state is the scope valve: pb would rather say the third verdict than let a double inflate the first._
- **DP-09** (v1) — The walk happens in a world with enough production-shaped data to be user-shaped — an account to sign in with, records to see and change — sourced from the system's own seeds and fixtures when present, with the origin of that data stated in the cast list. When the promised flow cannot be reached with available data, the verdict is 'couldn't determine: no way to reach this state,' never an empty-screen false failure and never quietly fabricated state passed off as real. — _A user-shaped walk needs a user and something to click; and data is the stand-in everyone forgets to disclose — synthetic state presented as real state inflates verdicts exactly like a fake payment API does._
- **DP-10** (v1) — Destruction is guaranteed by a deadman's switch, not by good behavior: when the run ends — verdict delivered, crash, Ctrl-C, laptop lid closed — everything conjured is destroyed (processes, ports, storage, networks), enforced by a watchdog keyed to the run being alive rather than by pb's own cleanup code getting to execute. Two consecutive runs never see each other; a crashed pb never leaves a zombie system behind. — _'Disposable' is a promise with the same weight as the verdict. One leaked world that corrupts tomorrow's run — or eats the laptop until a reboot — destroys beat 7 (trust compounds) faster than any wrong verdict; Testcontainers earned a decade of default-trust on exactly this guarantee._
- **DP-11** (v1) — Environment failure is a first-class verdict with its own receipt: when the world fails to come to life (build breaks, migration crashes, port conflict), the case file is still produced — COULDN'T DETERMINE, with the first causal error, the exact service and its last words, explicitly separated from any judgment of the change, and phrased like an ally ('this is not evidence against your change'). Never a bare timeout, never a shrugged stack trace. — _Beat 6 and the Rust-compiler standard: env failures are the single most common event in this product's life and the moment users decide the tool is on their side or in their way. Blaming the change for a broken world is the fastest way to teach users to ignore verdicts._
- **DP-12** (v1) — The recipe travels in the receipt: the case file records everything needed to conjure the same world again — code fingerprint, resolved dependency identities, seed data identity, each double's version and scripted behavior — and prints the one command that re-runs the same judgment against the same world. — _Beat 5 says replayable: a verdict that cannot be re-derived is testimony, not proof. The recipe is also what lets a skeptical teammate check the checker — the SQLite move of being our own first customer._
- **DP-13** (defer) — Instant worlds: warm pools, snapshots, and caching so the system is alive in seconds instead of minutes across repeated runs. — _The Catch survives an honestly-narrated three-minute conjure; it does not survive shipping late. Speed is a compounding investment to make after the straight line exists — deferred, not dismissed, because time-to-verdict eventually decides daily use._
- **DP-14** (defer) — The graduation ladder: run the same walk against sanctioned real vendors (user-supplied Stripe test-mode keys, trap inboxes for email), upgrading a qualified stand-in WORKS or a COULDN'T DETERMINE into a fully real verdict for integration-shaped promises. — _It is the honest long-term answer to DP-08's limits — but it drags in secrets handling and a config surface, both explicitly banned from v1. The verdict language of DP-08 is designed so this slot exists without v1 building it._
- **DP-15** (defer) — Learned doubles: record real third-party traffic once (with consent and credentials) and replay versioned, dated recordings as higher-fidelity stand-ins, with staleness visible in the cast list. — _Recording infrastructure, cassette hygiene, and staleness honesty form a product of their own; v1 ships a few curated doubles instead. Deferring this keeps v1's honesty claims small enough to actually be true._
- **DP-16** (defer) — Production-data twins: anonymized, subsetted snapshots of real production data as the conjured world's state, with provenance and privacy guarantees in the cast list. — _Realistic data catches a class of bugs seeds never will — but anonymization done wrong is a breach, not a feature. This is the highest-stakes trust surface in the vertical and deserves its own dedicated effort post-v1._
- **DP-17** (defer) — Many worlds: parallel conjured instances, long-lived shareable environments, preview links for teammates. — _Guardrail: ship the courtroom, not the legal system. One world per judgment, destroyed after, is the v1 shape; sharing and persistence reintroduce the dashboard/auth/history surface v1 explicitly cut._
- **DP-18** (defer) — Every system shape: Kubernetes-native topologies, serverless, exotic multi-repo constellations. v1 conjures one common multi-service shape brilliantly and answers everything else with 'couldn't determine: I can't bring this system shape to life yet, because X.' — _The guardrail names the multi-stack matrix as the classic start-but-never-deliver trap. The tri-state verdict is the pressure valve that lets v1 decline honestly instead of supporting everything badly._

**Learn from (quality bar):**

- **Testcontainers (+ its Ryuk reaper)** (https://testcontainers.com) — How 'real over mock' won culturally: they made real dependencies SAFER than fakes via two guarantees pb must replicate. (1) Destruction that survives your death — Ryuk is an independent watchdog that holds a liveness connection to the run and prunes everything the run created (matched by session labels) ~10s after that connection drops, so a SIGKILLed process still leaks nothing; cleanup never depends on the tool's own code path executing. (2) Readiness as a proven fact — wait strategies (answered HTTP 200, emitted the ready log line, passed a healthcheck) explicitly reject the port-is-open lie and separate 'process started' from 'genuinely ready to serve.' The compound lesson: a tool that never leaves a mess and never lies about 'ready' earns the right to be run reflexively, which is exactly the trust posture The Catch needs.
- **LocalStack** (https://github.com/localstack/localstack) — What it costs to be an honest stand-in — and why pb should mostly refuse to pay it. LocalStack measures its own gap from reality with snapshot 'parity tests' recorded against real AWS as ground truth (down to error-message wording, after learning a slightly-off ValidationException text broke real CDK deploys), publishes per-operation coverage, and defaults every unimplemented operation to a loud NotImplementedError instead of a plausible fake 200. Steal the posture, not the ambition: a double earns trust by quantifying and publishing its own fidelity and by failing loudly at the edge of its knowledge — and the sheer scale of their parity machinery is the argument for pb shipping few, curated, behavior-enumerated doubles rather than pretending to simulate the world.
- **MSW (Mock Service Worker)** (https://mswjs.io) — Where the honesty boundary belongs: at the wire, with the judged system untouched. MSW's system under test keeps making REAL requests with zero code modification — fakery is described as 'network behavior,' a contract at the boundary, never a patched call site — so what you observed is what the app actually does. And its strictness switch (onUnhandledRequest: 'error') is the seed of pb's sealed room: traffic nobody declared a double for fails LOUDLY instead of drifting to the real network or silently passing. The lesson: intercept where reality and the system meet, and make 'we never told the double about this request' an error with a name, not a shrug.

Detect and report as a finding, or hard-contain in v1?


## The Skeptical User
**Hardest-problem approach.** Never ask the model 'have you tested enough?' or 'does this work?' — both questions invite the documented failure mode (LLM evaluators recognize and favor their own kind's generations, with self-preference rising linearly with self-recognition; Panickssery et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2404.13076). Instead, remove both judgments from the model's discretion. (1) Make 'enough' a closed obligation set, not a feeling: promise → 3-7 pre-declared falsifiable claims × a fixed hostile-probe taxonomy = a finite checklist created before probing begins; 'enough' is definitionally that checklist executed. (2) The stopping rule is mechanical with exactly three exits: FALSIFIED — first catch that reproduces on immediate re-run ends the trial early, remaining budget goes to shrinking the repro; SATURATED — all obligations executed AND the last K probes surfaced no new behavior (new DOM state / response shape / console error), borrowing the fuzzing literature's saturation criterion since distinct new findings cost exponentially more probing (Green Fuzzing, ISSTA'23); BUDGET — wall-clock cap hit with obligations open, which can only render COULD NOT DETERMINE naming the untested claims. There is no exit labeled 'the agent feels confident.' (3) Separation of powers: the LLM plans probes and narrates; the VERDICT is computed by a deterministic rules engine over harness-captured receipts (HTTP statuses, pre-declared DOM assertions, data diffs, cross-surface equality). Where a perceptual judgment is unavoidable ('does this screen show the confirmation?'), it is a claim-specific binary question asked blind against a harness screenshot, and YES must quote the evidence it relies on; a verdict resting too heavily on perceptual judgments auto-degrades to couldn't-determine. (4) Blind prosecution: the skeptical user never sees the builder's diff commentary, commit messages, test names, or 'done' claims — only the promise and the running system; sycophancy needs the sibling's voice, and self-preference bias needs its fingerprints, so admit neither into evidence. (5) Burden inversion as the resting state: the verdict state machine starts at COULD NOT DETERMINE; only affirmative receipts move claims to SURVIVED; only reproduced falsifications move to DOES NOT WORK; WORKS is reachable solely when every claim is receipt-backed — so the lazy gradient points to honest ignorance, never to a false pass. (6) Measure the sycophancy out rather than assume it out: gate every judge build against a seeded-lie corpus (paired known-good / known-broken-in-a-specific-way builds of the same flows); a judge that blesses a seeded lie or indicts a seeded truth fails its own build, and catch-rate/false-alarm-rate are tracked like SQLite tracks its own test coverage. Honest bound, stated plainly: this machinery bounds the judge, it does not perfect it — every verdict prints its residual doubt ('we can prove the presence of bugs, not their absence'), and anything the machinery could not ground is surfaced as couldn't-determine rather than absorbed into a pass. The product's promise is not omniscience; it is that it never says WORKS beyond its receipts.

**Functional requirements:**

- **SU-01** (v1) — Charges before trial: within seconds of reading the one-sentence promise, the skeptical user announces the 3-7 falsifiable claims it will try to break ('For this to be true: a first-time visitor can reach the form; submitting persists a record; the record survives a refresh...') and that closed claim list is the fixed contract for the entire run. — _Makes 'enough probing' definable and auditable instead of a feeling, and lets the user catch a mis-read promise in the first five seconds — before 90 seconds are spent prosecuting the wrong charge._
- **SU-02** (v1) — Every probe pre-registers its kill condition: before acting, each probe states what it is trying to disconfirm and what failure would look like; a probe that cannot fail is not admitted to the record. — _Turns narration from demo-speak into prosecution, and structurally blocks the sycophancy vector of padding the run with vacuous confirmations that were never capable of catching anything._
- **SU-03** (v1) — Walks the UI as a stranger, not as the builder: reaches the feature the way a first-time user would — through the app's front door, no deep-links lifted from the diff, no builder-provided script — clicking and typing with human pacing and human mistakes. — _Agents pass their own tests by exercising exactly the code path they wrote; the stranger's path (broken nav, dead route, hidden precondition) is where the lies live._
- **SU-04** (v1) — Cross-examines every surface against the others: whenever the UI claims success it independently verifies the API response and the stored data agree (toast says saved → GET returns it → the row actually changed), and vice versa — a disagreement between surfaces is itself a falsification. — _The most common agent lie is surface-deep success (UI optimism with no persistence); cross-surface disagreement is objective, deterministic evidence that no judge — human or LLM — can be sweet-talked out of._
- **SU-05** (v1) — Runs the hostile-first-user repertoire on the one flow: a fixed, visible taxonomy of break attempts — wrong/empty/boundary input, double-submit, mid-flow refresh, back-button and out-of-order steps, and re-entry after completion — with inapplicable classes shown as N/A in the ledger, never silently skipped. — _These are the things real users do in the first five minutes and demos never do; a fixed taxonomy is what keeps v1's definition of 'probed' honest and auditable._
- **SU-06** (v1) — Verifies the courtroom before the trial: before any hostile probing, a control probe exercises an adjacent untouched flow (or the app shell); if the control fails, the verdict is COULD NOT DETERMINE (environment broken) — never DOES NOT WORK. — _A prosecutor who indicts in a broken courtroom destroys trust in one run; env-vs-change discrimination is the credibility floor under every catch._
- **SU-07** (v1) — One confirmed catch ends the trial: the first falsification stops further probing and the remaining budget is spent making it undeniable — the catch must reproduce on an immediate re-run before DOES NOT WORK is rendered (a one-off is reported as 'observed once, could not reproduce', with the recording), then the repro is shrunk to the shortest path from app-open to broken moment. — _This is the ~90-second magic moment, and reproduce-before-indict is the war on flake: one false catch and the retellable story ('it caught my agent lying') dies permanently._
- **SU-08** (v1) — WORKS must be earned, never defaulted: the verdict is computed from the probe record by deterministic rules — every claim needs affirmative harness-captured evidence AND survival of its mandatory hostile probes; anything less renders COULD NOT DETERMINE naming exactly which claim went untested and why. Absence of failure is never success. — _Inverts the sycophancy gradient: the lazy path now leads to honest ignorance instead of a false pass, and the tri-state verdict becomes the scope valve the guardrail demands._
- **SU-09** (v1) — The agent narrates, the harness testifies: all evidence (screenshots, requests/responses, data before/after, timestamps, fingerprint of the code that ran) is captured by the instrumented harness, never asserted by the agent; a narration sentence without a linked harness receipt does not render in the case file. — _An LLM can be sycophantic but it cannot fabricate receipts it does not control; separating the storyteller from the witness is the core anti-lying architecture of the whole product._
- **SU-10** (v1) — Live narration in break-intent language with a claim scoreboard: each action narrates as an attack ('Submitting twice fast — real users double-click; expecting exactly one record') while claims flip UNTESTED → SURVIVED / FALSIFIED in real time. — _Makes watching riveting (beat 3) and makes the narration BE the audit trail forming — the entertainment and the evidence are the same artifact, so neither can drift from the other._
- **SU-11** (v1) — The acquittal is auditable: the case file's verdict page shows the complete probe ledger — every attack attempted, what it tried to falsify, outcome, and receipt link — including the boring survivals. — _'Works' is only worth retelling if you can audit what it survived; Jepsen's lesson is that trust compounds through inspectable acquittals, not through green checkmarks._
- **SU-12** (v1) — States its residual doubt: even on WORKS, the verdict names in one honest paragraph what a skeptical human should still doubt ('not probed: concurrent users, permissions, mobile viewport'), in the register of 'we can prove the presence of bugs, not their absence'. — _Jepsen's standing disclaimer is precisely why its passes are believed; a verdict that implies omniscience poisons every future verdict the product renders._
- **SU-13** (v1) — The judge is itself judged: development and release of the skeptical user are gated by a seeded-lie fixture suite — paired known-good and known-broken-in-a-specific-way builds of the same flows — and a judge build that blesses a seeded lie or indicts a seeded truth cannot ship. — _You cannot engineer sycophancy out by intent, only measure it out; catch-rate on lies and pass-rate on truths are the only ground truth for the judge, and without this THE CATCH is luck (SQLite standard: be our own first customer)._
- **SU-14** (defer) — Adaptive, learned probe repertoire: probe selection weighted by diff risk, application shape, and past catches, growing beyond the fixed v1 taxonomy. — _Raises catch power over time, but an open-ended repertoire undermines the auditability that makes v1 verdicts honest; expand only after the fixed taxonomy has earned trust._
- **SU-15** (defer) — Multi-user and concurrency probing: two skeptical users interleaving on the same flow (simultaneous edits, session races) beyond v1's double-submit. — _A rich catch class, but requires session orchestration off the straight line to the magic moment; double-submit covers the highest-frequency slice in v1._
- **SU-16** (defer) — Hostile-attacker persona: authorization matrices, injection, IDOR, and other security probing. — _A different promise than 'a real user can walk through this feature'; mixing personas in v1 blurs the verdict's meaning and doubles the calibration burden._
- **SU-17** (defer) — Catch replay as standing regression memory: every confirmed catch becomes a permanently replayed first probe on future runs of the same promise (Hypothesis example-database pattern). — _This is history/ledger territory explicitly cut from v1, but it is the compounding-trust flywheel to build the moment v1 ships._
- **SU-18** (defer) — Human steering mid-trial: the watcher can whisper 'also try X' and have it admitted to the probe ledger as a first-class pre-registered probe. — _Delightful, but it is config surface (cut in v1) and it complicates the closed-claim-set stopping rule that keeps the verdict honest._

**Learn from (quality bar):**

- **Jepsen** (https://jepsen.io/analyses) — The acquittal-credibility playbook: every report pairs receipt-backed findings with a standing epistemic boundary — 'we can prove the presence of bugs, but not their absence' — plus an explicit section naming what was NOT tested (e.g., Datomic report §4.5) and links to raw, re-runnable histories. That is why a Jepsen verdict moves vendors while their own green dashboards don't. Steal: a verdict that names its own limits is the only kind that compounds trust, and adversarial findings stay credible even under vendor funding when methodology and evidence are fully public.
- **Hypothesis** (https://github.com/HypothesisWorks/hypothesis) — The falsification engine's manners: it exists to break your property, and when it cannot probe rigorously it FAILS LOUDLY via health checks (filter_too_much, too_slow, data_too_large) instead of handing you a hollow green; on a catch it shrinks to the minimal counterexample and replays it first on every future run. Steal: make 'I couldn't really test this' a first-class loud outcome rather than a silent pass — the exact anti-sycophancy pattern — and treat the minimal repro as the true deliverable of failure.
- **Schemathesis** (https://github.com/schemathesis/schemathesis) — Hostile-first-user adoption craft for API surfaces: one command against a live schema yields deduplicated findings, each with a copy-paste curl reproduction; default checks are disconfirmation-shaped (500s, schema violations, validation bypass, stateful bugs); adoption earned via instant time-to-first-catch plus third-party proof (1.4-4.5x more defects than peer tools in academic evaluation; Spotify/Red Hat/JetBrains use it). Steal: time-to-first-catch is the adoption engine, and a receipt the human can re-fire in one paste is what makes a catch believed rather than argued with.

**Open questions:** Claim decomposition under ambiguity: when the one-sentence promise is vague, does the skeptical user ask exactly one clarifying question before trial, or decompose conservatively and mark ambiguous claims couldn't-determine? Who owns the final wording of the claims — the tool or the user?; What is the concrete, cheap, non-gameable novelty fingerprint for the saturation signal in a mixed UI+API run — new DOM state hashes, new response shapes, new console/network error classes, or some composite — and what K (probes-without-novelty) is right for a 90-second budget?; Which claim types genuinely require a perceptual LLM judgment versus a deterministic assertion, and what share of a verdict may rest on perceptual judgments before it auto-degrades to COULD NOT DETERMINE?; Reproduce-before-indict versus real races: a genuine 1-in-5 race is a real bug — how many re-runs before rendering 'observed once, could not reproduce', and how is that verdict phrased so it reads as unresolved suspicion rather than an acquittal?; Seeded-lie corpus design: which lie taxonomy (UI-optimism/no-persistence, dead route, partial rollout, wrong-entity edit, validation theater...) and how many fixtures make a credible v1 gate — and do we publish the corpus SQLite-style at v1 or after?; Model independence: is blinding the prober sufficient, or should probe-planning and perceptual judging run on a different model family than typical builder agents to further cut self-preference — and what does that cost the 90-second wall-clock budget?; Budget split: what default wall-clock cap lets THE CATCH land in ~90 seconds while saturation on a truthful build doesn't stretch to 10+ minutes, and how is remaining budget divided between trial, reproduction, and repro-shrinking?; Front-door discovery limits: when the stranger's path to the feature cannot be found from the app shell within budget (feature behind unknown nav), is that itself a falsification ('a real user cannot find it'), a couldn't-determine, or a prompt-time question?


## Unfakeable Evidence
**Hardest-problem approach.** Adopt SLSA's central move wholesale: the party that benefits from lying must be ARCHITECTURALLY unable to touch the evidence — not forbidden, unable. pb has three suspects: (a) the agent-authored code under test, (b) the LLM that narrates and judges, (c) pb's own binary and host. v1 neutralizes (a) and (b) by construction and honestly bounds (c). The construction is 'recorder beneath, judge after, renderer between'. RECORDER: deterministic, non-LLM code holding the ONLY write handle to an append-only evidence store. It observes at layers beneath both the app and the acting user — pixels from the browser engine, traffic at pb's own wire tap, rows via direct read-only DB connection, and code fingerprints read off the live runtime (image digests, commit + dirty-tree hash), never off the agent's claim. Every observation becomes a typed receipt: content-fingerprinted, chained to the previous receipt in capture order, stamped with a monotonic clock, labeled with its capture layer (first-party vs hearsay). Agent code can print SUCCESS all day; it cannot reach the layers receipts come from. JUDGE (the LLM) starts only after the recorder SEALS the store — computes the chain head, writes a manifest (count + root), drops its own write access, and prints EVIDENCE SEALED <root> to the terminal before JUDGING appears. The judge's entire I/O surface is: read sealed receipts, emit prose plus receipt-ID citations. There is no tool in its hands that creates, edits, or deletes evidence — fabrication is not a behavior to discourage, it is a channel that does not exist, exactly as SLSA keeps signing material out of tenant reach. RENDERER: deterministic again, the constitutional court. It builds the HTML case file by inlining receipt content only via hash lookup from the sealed store; judge prose can only point. Three mechanical rules: a citation that doesn't resolve renders as 'CITED EVIDENCE NOT IN RECORD' and auto-degrades the verdict to couldn't-honestly-determine; verdict-bearing sentences without citations are flagged UNCITED; hearsay-only support for a verdict claim is rejected. It also reports citation coverage, so judicial OMISSION (ignoring a failing receipt) is surfaced mechanically, not left to the judge's conscience. The file embeds the sealed root and an offline WebCrypto self-verifier — one click re-audits every hash with no network. THE HONEST BOUND (where we refuse to lie): a local hash chain makes the record tamper-evident after sealing and makes the judge unable to fabricate; it does NOT prove to a third party that pb's binary or the host wasn't compromised, nor that the recorder saw everything — that tier needs signatures and an external transparency log (deferred, named in the case file's 'what this seal proves / cannot prove' panel, in SLSA-threat-table style). The backstop for the residual trust gap is replay, not rhetoric: the case file embeds the exact walk and fingerprints, so a doubter re-runs it against the same system and mints their own receipts — per the transparent-log lesson, the goal is an operator who is 'essentially untrusted' because checking is cheaper than trusting.

**Functional requirements:**

- **EV-1** (v1) — Total capture is the default: from the second a run starts, everything the skeptical user's walk touches — every screenshot, every HTTP request/response, every DB before/after snapshot, every console/log line, every step taken — becomes a receipt automatically, with zero flags, zero setup, zero instrumentation of the target app. — _The Catch dies if the broken moment happens off-camera. Playwright proved capture must cost nothing to be trusted to exist when you need it; here it is not even a flag — a run with gaps in its record cannot honestly render a verdict._
- **EV-2** (v1) — Code identity is observed, never asserted: the run opens by fingerprinting the system that is actually running (container image digests from the runtime, commit + dirty-tree hash of the code it brought to life, checksums of served bundles), and the case file headline reads 'this verdict applies to exactly <fingerprint>'. If what the agent claimed it shipped differs from what is running, that mismatch is a headline finding, not a footnote. — _'Done' claims routinely get verified against the wrong build — stale container, uncommitted file, cached bundle. Binding the verdict to the exact code that ran is this vertical's core promise; without it every other receipt is testimony about an unknown defendant._
- **EV-3** (v1) — Discovery closes before deliberation, visibly: the user watches the terminal print 'EVIDENCE SEALED <root fingerprint> (N receipts)' BEFORE the word JUDGING ever appears. The case file proves the verdict was rendered over that sealed record and nothing else. — _This is the separation of powers made into a felt ritual. The one moment that convinces a skeptic is seeing that the record was closed before the judge opened its mouth — and the terminal-printed seal is an out-of-band anchor a doctored HTML file cannot retroactively match._
- **EV-4** (v1) — Every verdict-bearing sentence carries a live citation: click any claim in the narration or verdict and land on the exact receipt (the screenshot, the request, the data diff). A sentence with no citation is typographically flagged UNCITED; a citation that fails to resolve against the sealed record automatically degrades the whole verdict to 'couldn't honestly determine' with the defect shown. — _This converts prose into proof and makes judge hallucination structurally harmless: the LLM can only point at evidence, and pointing at nothing is itself caught by deterministic code. It is the courtroom rule — assertions without exhibits don't reach the jury._
- **EV-5** (v1) — Every receipt declares its capture layer in plain language — 'observed at the wire', 'read directly from the database', 'pixels from the browser', versus 'the app's own log line (hearsay)'. Hearsay may color the narration but can never be the sole support for a verdict-bearing claim. — _Agent-authored code printing SUCCESS is precisely the lie this product exists to catch. First-party-vs-hearsay labeling is how the case file stays honest about which observations the system under test could have faked and which it could not._
- **EV-6** (v1) — Before/after data receipts are provably ordered: state snapshots are chained in capture order so 'before' is demonstrably before the action and 'after' demonstrably after — the case file can show 'the order did not exist, the button was clicked, the order now exists' as a sequence that cannot be silently reordered or thinned. — _'The data actually changed' is the load-bearing claim of an end-to-end walk across services. Without tamper-evident ordering, a before/after pair is just two screenshots in a trench coat._
- **EV-7** (v1) — The case file verifies itself, offline: one click inside the single HTML file recomputes every receipt fingerprint and every chain link in the browser — no network, no install, nothing leaves the machine — and flips a banner to INTACT or TAMPERED (naming exactly which receipt broke). — _A receipt you can only look at is a picture; a receipt the recipient can re-audit with zero infrastructure is proof. This is trace.playwright.dev's client-side craft applied to integrity: the person the file gets forwarded to is the one who needs the check._
- **EV-8** (v1) — Every case file carries an honesty panel: a plain-language 'What this seal proves / What it cannot prove' section — it proves the judge could not fabricate or alter evidence and that the file hasn't changed since sealing; it does not prove the host machine wasn't compromised or that the recorder saw everything. The path to stronger claims (signed, externally logged) is named as future, not implied as present. — _Never lying includes never overclaiming. SLSA's threat tables ('SLSA v1.0 does not address this threat') and CT's tamper-evident-not-tamper-proof stance are why security people trust them; a trust product that inflates its own guarantee is dead on first audit._
- **EV-9** (v1) — The broken moment is a handoff-ready repro receipt: on DOES NOT WORK, the failing step's full bundle — exact step and inputs, request + response, state before/after, screenshot, code fingerprint — is extractable as one machine-readable block an agent can consume to reproduce the failure, and the case file embeds the entire walk so it can be re-run against the same system to mint a fresh, comparable case file. — _The retell is 'it caught my agent lying and showed me the receipt' — and the same receipt is the fix's starting point. Re-runnability is also the ultimate audit: anyone who doubts the record can go get their own receipts instead of trusting ours._
- **EV-10** (v1) — Receipts never leak credentials: obvious secrets (auth headers, cookies, tokens, connection strings) are masked at capture time, and each masking is itself receipted — with a fingerprint of the unmasked original retained — so redaction is visible and can never be a place to hide tampering. — _Case files exist to be shared; one API key leaked in a forwarded HTML file destroys more trust than a hundred correct verdicts build. Receipted redaction keeps the file shareable without opening an invisible hole in the evidence chain._
- **EV-11** (v1) — 'Couldn't honestly determine' gets the same evidentiary rigor as failure: the case file shows receipts for WHY — the service that never came up, the fingerprint that couldn't be established, the flaky dependency — so the third verdict state reads as a documented finding about the environment, never as a shrug. — _The tri-state verdict is the product's scope valve, and it only holds if the third state is as evidenced as the other two. An unreceipted 'couldn't determine' teaches users it means 'broken tool'; a receipted one teaches them it means 'honest witness'._
- **EV-12** (defer) — Third-party attestable verdicts: keyless signatures over the sealed record plus an append-only external transparency log, so someone who wasn't in the room can verify who ran the verdict, when, and that it was never re-minted. — _This is the Sigstore/Rekor tier of trust — right for teams and CI gates, but key management and log infrastructure are off the straight line to The Catch. v1's audience is the developer at the moment of doubt; local tamper-evidence plus re-runnability covers them._
- **EV-13** (defer) — Full deterministic session replay: scrub through the recorded walk offline with time-travel fidelity beyond screenshots and snapshots (rr-grade determinism of the system under test). — _Seductive and a research program. v1's embedded walk + fingerprints already deliver 'replayable' at the honest level — re-execute against the live system and compare case files — without promising determinism no multi-service sandbox can keep._
- **EV-14** (defer) — Cross-run evidence ledger: verdict history per flow, diffs between case files over time, trust curves. — _Explicitly cut by the guardrail (no history/ledger). The compounding-trust beat emerges from many good single case files first; a ledger of mediocre evidence compounds nothing._
- **EV-15** (defer) — Attested runner environment (TEE / remote attestation) so even a compromised host cannot forge the record. — _Beyond v1's stated threat model — and stating that boundary out loud in the honesty panel (EV-8) is worth more trust in v1 than half-shipping enclave support._

**Learn from (quality bar):**

- **SLSA provenance + in-toto attestations** (https://slsa.dev/spec/v1.0/threats) — 'Unforgeable' evidence is achieved by construction, not by policy: provenance is generated by the platform, physically out of reach of the user-defined build steps — 'the build project owner must not have the ability to influence the build process or provenance generation', and 'only the control plane has access to cryptographic secrets'. Every claim is bound to artifacts by digest, and separation of duties (in-toto layouts: only Alice may tag a release) is enforced by verification, not by trust. Equally stealable: the threat tables that say plainly 'SLSA v1.0 does not address this threat' — enumerating what you do NOT protect against is what makes security people believe the rest.
- **Sigstore (cosign + Rekor), on Certificate Transparency's foundations** (https://docs.sigstore.dev/logging/overview/) — Adoption came from deleting friction from verification, not from adding cryptography: one command, zero key management ('the Let's Encrypt of code signing'), which is why npm and PyPI moved. And its trust statement is stated at exactly its true strength — the log is tamper-EVIDENT, not tamper-proof; per Russ Cox's transparent-log design, the operator becomes 'essentially untrusted' because a lie must be maintained 'forever serving an alternate log containing the lie'. Steal both: verifying a case file must be one click with zero infrastructure, and every integrity claim in the product must be phrased at precisely the strength it actually has.
- **Playwright Trace Viewer** (https://playwright.dev/docs/trace-viewer) — The evidence artifact IS the product experience: the trace is recorded by the runtime BENEATH the test code (user code cannot write the trace — the exact recorder/actor separation this vertical needs), it is one self-contained file with before/after state per action plus network, console, and source in a single timeline, and it opens by drag-drop in a fully client-side viewer where data never leaves the browser. Steal: capture invisible and complete by default, single-file portability, offline self-explanation — a failure artifact so good people share it instead of describing bugs in words.

**Open questions:** Judge omission vs fabrication: fabrication is solved by construction, but does the judge read ALL receipts or a deterministic index/digest of them? Big walks may exceed context — decide the summarization contract so that an ignored failing receipt is mechanically surfaced (renderer-computed 'uncited anomalies' list) rather than trusted to the judge's diligence.; Fingerprint floor for a green verdict: when code identity cannot be fully established (dirty tree, unpinned image, no digest available), does WORKS get capped to 'couldn't honestly determine', or issued with a loudly degraded binding? Pick one and make it a stated rule — this is the single biggest overclaim risk in the vertical.; Wire-tap honesty for TLS: v1's proxy sees traffic because pb itself routes the walk through it — how do we receipt the tap's presence ('all traffic observed at pb's proxy; the app saw pb's certificate') so first-party network evidence never quietly implies we observed traffic we didn't (e.g., service-to-service calls that bypassed the tap)?; Secrets found after sealing: masking happens at capture time, but if a secret is detected post-seal the record is immutable — is the remedy a re-run, or a derived redacted case file that references (and fingerprints) the sealed original? Never silent rewriting.; Receipt volume budget for a single self-contained HTML: what is the size ceiling, and what is the honest truncation policy — truncated bodies must say TRUNCATED and retain the full-content fingerprint so the chain still verifies?; Actor intent as evidence: does 'I clicked the Checkout button' count as first-party (the actor is pb's own deterministic driver) — and when the actor mis-targets, is the DOM-snapshot-at-click-coordinates the arbiter that keeps actor drift from masquerading as an app bug?; Cross-service ordering: before/after claims that span multiple services can't lean on wall clocks — is recorder-capture-order sufficient for v1's claim language, and how do we phrase ordering honestly when two effects were observed near-simultaneously from different taps?


## The Honest Verdict — the tri-state judgment (WORKS / DOES NOT WORK / COULD NOT DETERMINE) with 30-second human readability
**Hardest-problem approach.** Stance: uncertainty is the DEFAULT state of every run, and only evidence — never judgment, never the narrator — moves a run out of it. The verdict is a pure function of a recorded evidence ledger, computed by boring deterministic rules; the LLM that drives and narrates the walk is untrusted by design and cannot flip the verdict (it colors the story, the judge reads only receipts). Six concrete moves: (1) ASYMMETRIC BURDEN OF PROOF, ENCODED AS REACHABILITY. WORKS is the hardest state to enter: every promised step observed succeeding through the real entry path, the promise's end-state assertion observed, the full receipt chain present, zero unresolved anomalies. DOES NOT WORK has one narrow gate: a receipt-backed failure of a promised step inside the system under judgment that survives the determinism gate. Every other run state — env blockage, harness fault, missing receipts, expired budget, ambiguous promise, unattributable inconsistency — lands in COULD NOT DETERMINE by construction. 'Never rounded up to pass' is thus structural: pass isn't a rounding target, it's a chain you either completed or didn't. (2) THREE-WAY BLAME BEFORE THE TRI-STATE. Every anomaly is attributed SUBJECT / ENVIRONMENT / HARNESS by mechanical position rules: failures before the walk's first promised step reaches the system = environment (→ CND naming the dependency, with its receipt); failures inside pb's own tooling stack = harness (→ first-person confession, own exit code, can never impersonate a verdict — pytest's exit-3 and Bazel's infrastructure-failure file prove blame can be protocol); failures inside the promised walk on the product's own surfaces = subject-candidate, which alone may become DOES NOT WORK. (3) DETERMINISM GATE INSTEAD OF RETRY-UNTIL-GREEN (Hypothesis's move). A subject-candidate failure triggers exactly 2 identical replays from the same starting state — purpose: measure consistency, not seek green. All attempts are first-class evidence, shown side by side. Fails 3/3 at the same moment → DOES NOT WORK, deterministic, repro attached. Mixed results where the inconsistent step is the promise's own user-visible behavior → DOES NOT WORK — intermittent, with observed rate ('a real user hits this 1 in 3 times'); mixed results rooted in env/harness territory, or a walk that cannot be replayed from the same state (irreversible mutation) → CND naming exactly that. A later green never erases an earlier red — this is the precise refusal of Bazel's FLAKY-counts-as-passed rollup. (4) EARN THE RIGHT TO CALL FLAKE MEANINGFUL by killing harness-induced flake at the source: no sleeps, event- and actionability-driven waiting (Playwright's war on flake), a fresh deterministic starting state per attempt, pinned fingerprints for code and env. Calibration is impossible on a wobbling instrument; most 'flake' in the wild is harness flake, so this is where the engineering budget goes, and it's what makes residual nondeterminism attributable to the system rather than to us. (5) CALIBRATE EMPIRICALLY, IN PUBLIC (the SQLite move). Ship and maintain a seeded ground-truth corpus — agent-broke-it changes, genuinely-done changes, sabotaged environments (dep down, bad creds, empty fixtures), planted intermittence — and hold hard, asymmetric budgets: false-WORKS on known-broken = 0, release-blocking, forever (the one unforgivable error); known-good must yield WORKS ≥ ~80% with misses landing in CND (false DOES-NOT-WORK ≤ ~5%, because false alarms burn trust almost as fast as false passes); sabotaged-env cases must land CND-with-correct-blame ≥ ~90%; overall CND on the corpus budgeted at ~15–25% — above that the tool is honest but useless, and the ONLY permitted fixes are hardening the harness and evidence capture, never loosening the WORKS bar. Publish the confusion matrix in the repo on every release. (6) NO NUMBERS ON INDIVIDUAL VERDICTS. No '0.87 confidence' — categorical verdict + named reasons + receipts; numeric confidence invites both rounding and false precision, and calibration lives in the public corpus statistics where it can be audited, not as per-run decoration. Honest bound on the whole approach: this design does not make the verdict infallible — it makes it auditable, recomputable from receipts by a skeptical human, and structurally biased toward confessing uncertainty over inventing certainty. It can be wrong; it cannot quietly lie.

**Functional requirements:**

- **HV-1** (v1) — Every run ends in exactly one of three verdicts — WORKS, DOES NOT WORK, COULD NOT DETERMINE — mutually exclusive, no scores, no percentages, no 'mostly passed', no fourth hedge state. All ambiguity is forced into the third verdict's reason, never smeared across the other two. — _A judgment you can retell in one word is the product. The moment the verdict becomes a dial instead of a gavel, the user is back to interpreting output themselves — the job we exist to take._
- **HV-2** (v1) — The verdict block (last thing in the terminal, first thing in the case file) is readable in 30 seconds: the verdict word, the promise restated in the tool's own words, one plain-language sentence naming the decisive moment ('clicking Pay returned an empty cart'), whose side the problem is on, and the single strongest receipt one glance/click away. — _This is the retell artifact of The Catch: 'it said DOES NOT WORK and showed me the broken screen.' If the verdict takes scrolling to understand, the magic moment dies in the demo._
- **HV-3** (v1) — WORKS is scoped and confessional: it states exactly what walk was performed (steps taken, services touched, data used) and explicitly names what was NOT checked. It always reads as 'this walk succeeded on this build' — never as 'the feature is correct.' — _Jepsen's credibility comes from never claiming more than the evidence: 'we cannot prove correctness, only find errors.' The first time WORKS overclaims and a user gets burned, trust stops compounding permanently._
- **HV-4** (v1) — Every DOES NOT WORK is a falsifiable claim: it names the exact broken step in user terms and binds it to a specific receipt (the screen, the request/response, the data before/after) such that a skeptical reader can inspect the receipt and legitimately disagree with the verdict. — _The party under suspicion is an agent; the verdict is only worth more than the agent's word if it rests on evidence rather than testimony. Falsifiability is what makes it a judgment instead of another opinion._
- **HV-5** (v1) — Evidence-completeness gates the pass: WORKS is claimable only when the full receipt chain for every promised step exists. If the walk visually succeeded but evidence capture failed (missing screenshot, unrecorded response), the verdict demotes to COULD NOT DETERMINE — 'walked it, but can't prove it.' — _This makes 'never rounds up to pass' structural rather than aspirational: proof, not experience of the run, is the currency. Green must be as auditable as red or the whole receipt promise is theater._
- **HV-6** (v1) — COULD NOT DETERMINE is never a shrug — it always delivers a fixed contract: (a) what it was trying to establish, (b) the specific obstacle with its receipt, (c) whose side the obstacle is on (environment / harness / the promise itself being too ambiguous to execute), (d) the smallest action that would unblock it, and (e) the exact re-run command. — _CND is only honest if it is also useful. A CND the user can convert to a real verdict in one action keeps 'could not determine' from becoming the verdict people learn to ignore — the calibration failure mode._
- **HV-7** (v1) — Blame separation is part of the verdict, not commentary: DOES NOT WORK asserts the promised flow broke inside the system under judgment after being reached through the real entry path; a dependency/sandbox/data blockage lands in CND naming the dependency; and when pb itself fails, it confesses in first person ('my fault — pb hit an internal error; your change was not judged') and that state can never masquerade as any of the three verdicts. — _This is beat 6 of the ideal experience — 'your change is wrong' vs 'the env was broken' — and it's what pytest (exit 3 = internal error) and Bazel (a dedicated infrastructure-failure channel) prove can be protocol rather than vibes. A tool that blames the user for its own crashes is the Rust-compiler bar inverted._
- **HV-8** (v1) — Nondeterminism is surfaced, never absorbed: any failure candidate triggers a small, pre-declared number of identical replays from the same starting state to measure consistency — never to seek green. All attempts are kept and shown side by side; a flow that fails intermittently is reported as DOES NOT WORK — intermittent with the observed rate ('failed 2 of 3 identical walks at the same step'). A later green never erases an earlier red. — _Retry-until-green is the industry's institutionalized lie (Bazel marks pass-on-retry FLAKY, then counts it as passed anyway — its own users call that out). A user who hits a 1-in-3 failure experiences a broken product; the intermittence IS the finding._
- **HV-9** (v1) — Every verdict is bound to a fingerprint: the exact code judged (commit SHA / image digest), the environment identity, the timestamp, and the case file's own content hash appear with the verdict. The verdict reads as a claim about THIS build at THIS moment, and the fingerprint is what makes it citable in a PR or a standup. — _'The agent said done' is unfalsifiable partly because it's about nothing in particular. Binding the judgment to an exact artifact is what turns 'it works' into a receipt-backed statement someone can stake a ship decision on — and what prevents provenance games._
- **HV-10** (v1) — The machine contract mirrors the human one: distinct, documented, frozen-from-day-one exit codes for WORKS / DOES NOT WORK / COULD NOT DETERMINE / pb-internal-failure. A crash of the tool can never exit 0 and can never impersonate a verdict. — _The first thing users do after the demo is put the CLI in a script. If the exit codes are an afterthought (or a crash looks like a pass), the honesty guarantee evaporates exactly where nobody is watching._
- **HV-11** (v1) — The verdict is recomputable by the reader: it derives mechanically from the recorded evidence (observed step outcomes, replay consistency, blame attribution), and the case file shows which pieces of evidence drove it. The narrator's prose colors the story but can never flip the verdict; a skeptical reader can re-derive the same judgment from the receipts alone. — _The product's premise is that agent self-report is untrusted — so the judge cannot be another untrusted agent. Separating the storyteller from the judge is the single stance that makes 'it caught my agent lying' credible rather than 'one AI's word against another's.'_
- **HV-12** (v1) — Time-budget honesty: if the run's budget expires mid-walk, the verdict is COULD NOT DETERMINE — 'ran out of time at step 4 of 7' — with all completed steps' evidence retained and the unblocking action being a longer budget. Partial progress never rounds up to WORKS and never rounds down to DOES NOT WORK. — _A ~90-second product needs a designed answer for the walk that doesn't fit in 90 seconds; without one, timeouts silently become whichever verdict is most convenient, which is the exact lie we exist to end._
- **HV-13** (v1) — The product publicly holds itself to a seeded ground-truth corpus: known-broken changes, genuinely-done changes, sabotaged environments, and planted intermittence, with the resulting confusion matrix (including the false-WORKS count, which must be zero) published in the repo and re-run on every release. — _This is the SQLite move — trust earned via obsessive public self-testing — applied to the only question adopters actually have: 'how do I know YOUR verdict isn't lying?' It is also the only way to keep 'could not determine' calibrated over time instead of drifting toward either cowardice or bravado._
- **HV-14** (defer) — Baseline exoneration run: on a DOES NOT WORK, walk the same promise against the unchanged system to prove 'your change broke this' vs 'this was already broken before you.' — _The strongest possible blame upgrade — but it doubles runtime and requires bringing up a second system; v1 stays honest without it by phrasing blame as 'broke at step N on this build' and CND-ing 'whether this predates your change.'_
- **HV-15** (defer) — Verdict history and trend awareness ('this flow has gone intermittent 3 times this week', 'first failure in 40 runs'). — _Real calibration power, but it is the ledger — explicitly cut by the guardrail. One run must stand alone first._
- **HV-16** (defer) — Numeric confidence attached to individual verdicts (e.g., 'WORKS, 0.87'). — _Deliberately deferred and possibly forever: numbers invite rounding and false precision. In v1, calibration lives in the public corpus statistics; each verdict stays categorical with named reasons._
- **HV-17** (defer) — Configurable verdict policy (replay budgets, what counts as intermittent, per-team strictness). — _No config surface in v1 per the guardrail; one opinionated default keeps the verdict's meaning identical across every retelling, which is what lets 'done means done' become shared vocabulary._
- **HV-18** (defer) — Aggregated multi-promise verdicts (a suite of promises rolled into one report). — _One run = one promise = one verdict is the shape of the magic moment; aggregation reintroduces the dashboard the guardrail cut._
- **HV-19** (defer) — Structured machine-readable verdict document (JSON) for CI/PR-bot consumption beyond exit codes. — _The CI/PR bot is explicitly out of v1; exit codes carry the day-one scripting need without committing to a schema before the verdict's semantics have hardened._

**Learn from (quality bar):**

- **Jepsen** (https://jepsen.io/analyses) — The verdict as an evidence-bounded claim — and tri-state as first-class. Jepsen's checker literally returns :valid? true / false / :unknown, and its entire reputation rests on epistemic discipline: 'we cannot prove correctness, only find errors'; bugs are 'observable in production, not theoretical'; every conclusion is scoped to an exact version and configuration; errors are invited via a public errata address. Steal the posture: a pass is 'no violation observed on this walk of this build,' a fail is a reproducible observation with the history attached, and 'unknown' is a legitimate, respectable answer when evidence is insufficient — not an embarrassment to be hidden. Jepsen is proof that a tool whose verdicts people quote in purchasing decisions earns that by underclaiming.
- **Hypothesis** (https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial/flaky.html) — Nondeterminism is a named, first-class finding — never something you retry away. When a failure doesn't reproduce on identical replay, Hypothesis raises FlakyFailure as an error carrying the ORIGINAL failure inside it, because 'flakiness makes debugging failures substantially harder' and determinism is what makes its replay database (exact failing input, replayed automatically next run) possible. Steal the stance and the mechanics: identical-replay as the test of a failure's reality, both attempts preserved as evidence, the nondeterminism itself surfaced with its own name and its own explanation of what to do about it. This is the exact anti-'retry-until-green' architecture the verdict needs.
- **Bazel (test result model)** (https://bazel.build/reference/test-encyclopedia) — A lesson and an anti-lesson in one project. Lesson: verdict vocabulary and blame channels can be protocol, not judgment — pass-on-retry is labeled FLAKY (a distinct status, never silently PASSED), and TEST_INFRASTRUCTURE_FAILURE_FILE is a dedicated channel for 'the infrastructure failed, not the test,' explicitly forbidden as a dumping ground for ordinary flakiness. Anti-lesson: Bazel then counts FLAKY as passed for the exit code, and its own community filed 'the behavior of --flaky_test_attempts is surprising and suboptimal' (issue #3783) — the honest label undone by a dishonest rollup. Steal the taxonomy and the infra-blame channel; refuse the final rounding-up, and keep the human-visible verdict and the machine-visible exit code telling the same truth.

**Open questions:** Subject boundary: is the product's own infrastructure (its gateway, its queue, its DB) part of the subject or the environment? A 502 from the app's own edge — DOES NOT WORK (a user doesn't care whose 502 it is) or CND? Lean subject, but the rule must be decided and published, because it moves verdicts between states.; Genuinely ambiguous intermittence: when 1-of-3 identical walks fails at a step that is promise-visible but smells environmental (e.g., a timeout on the product's own API), does it ship as DOES NOT WORK — intermittent or CND-with-two-suspects? Where exactly is the tiebreak written down?; Replay cost vs the 90-second moment: the determinism gate can triple runtime on failures. Replay the whole walk (simple, honest) or from a checkpointed state (fast, but checkpoint fidelity becomes a new lie surface)? Does the ~90s promise apply only to the clean-pass path?; Does WORKS require the skeptic's adversarial probes (pokes beyond the literal promised path) to also pass, or are probe findings side-notes that cannot flip the verdict in v1? This defines the line between the narration vertical and the judgment vertical.; Ambiguous promise sentences ('users can export reports' — which users? what format?): interpret-and-confess-the-reading-in-the-verdict-scope vs ask-before-running, and does 'promise too vague to execute' deserve its own named CND sub-reason with the candidate readings listed?; Without the deferred baseline run (HV-14), what is the honest v1 phrasing for 'this may have been broken before your change' — a standing caveat on every DOES NOT WORK, or only when heuristics suggest the failing surface predates the diff?; Exit-code taxonomy freeze: 0=WORKS, 1=DOES NOT WORK, 2=COULD NOT DETERMINE, 3=pb internal failure — does 'promise unexecutable as stated' fold into 2 or warrant its own code? This is a day-one public contract and cannot change later.; Calibration corpus parameters: are 0 false-WORKS / ≥80% recall-on-good / ≤5% false-alarm / 15–25% CND the right launch budgets, and what corpus size (dozens? hundreds of seeded scenarios?) makes those percentages statistically meaningful rather than decorative?


## The Trust Ledger
**Hardest-problem approach.** Stance: a verdict never decays as a FACT — only as a claim about NOW. So the ledger stores immutable facts and computes freshness at read time; it never mutates, expires, or 'ages out' a verdict record. Concretely: (1) BIND AT WRITE TIME, BY OBSERVATION. The skeptical walk itself discovers the promise's dependency slice: the harness records a touched-surface manifest — every service, route, queue, and table the walk actually crossed — with a content fingerprint for each (image digest, commit SHA, schema hash, env fingerprint). No hand-declared dependency graph, ever: what the walk touched IS the promise's world, and it is observed, not asserted. This ships in v1 inside every case file (in-toto-style: subjects identified by digest, never by branch or 'latest'), so decay handling later is a pure function over records that already exist. (2) COMPUTE FRESHNESS AT READ TIME, IN A TRI-STATE THAT MIRRORS THE VERDICT VALVE. Diff the verdict's touched-set fingerprints against the current system: STILL BINDING (every touched fingerprint byte-identical — the exact bytes the walk exercised are what runs today), STALE (name precisely what moved and how much of the walk it intersects: 'svc-b abc123→9f8e7d; 2 of 14 exercised requests crossed svc-b'), or CAN'T TELL (a touched surface has no observable fingerprint — third-party API, mutable data, out-of-band config — and we name which one). No TTLs, no fourth state, no '92% confident': time alone never stales a verdict, and 'fresh' is claimed only on byte-identical evidence. Every freshness answer carries its own receipt — what was compared, what couldn't be seen — so the decay judgment is as auditable as the verdict. (3) INVALIDATE BY INTERSECTION, AND WHEN FORCED, OVER-INVALIDATE. A deploy the walk never touched cannot stale it. Granularity starts at whole-service digest — any change to a touched service stales the verdict — accepting over-invalidation on purpose, because the Bazel lesson is asymmetric: an under-hashed input produces a falsely-green cached PASS (the unforgivable lie), while over-invalidation merely costs a 90-second re-run. Finer change→route slicing is a later optimization that must prove it can never claim STILL BINDING falsely before it's allowed to speak. (4) REPAIR, DON'T RE-VERIFY THE WORLD. Staleness produces a ranked smallest-repair plan (cargo-vet's 'suggest' move): 'one 90s re-run of the checkout walk restores 4 of 6 stale promises' — run at the moments of doubt the product already owns (pre-release, post-deploy), never as a background treadmill that turns the ledger into noise. (5) THE BOUND, SAID OUT LOUD: pb can promise 'this exact byte-configuration was proven to work, and here is exactly what has changed since.' It can never honestly promise 'nothing else could have broken it' — drifting data and unfingerprintable externals map permanently to CAN'T TELL with the culprit named. That is the same scope valve that keeps v1 shippable, applied to time: the ledger never lies, because everything it cannot see, it says it cannot see.

**Functional requirements:**

- **TL-1** (v1) — Every run mints one durable verdict record, and the self-contained case file IS that record: the promise sentence, the tri-state judgment, the timestamp, and the fingerprints of the exact code that ran — readable offline, in full, years later, with zero services running. — _The Catch is only retellable if the receipt survives the moment — a file you can attach to Slack, a PR, or a postmortem and it still proves itself. A verdict that lives in a process's stdout evaporates; a verdict that lives in a file starts compounding._
- **TL-2** (v1) — Every verdict has a stable, citable identity: a short, content-derived case ID printed by the CLI and inside the file. The file self-verifies — anyone holding it can confirm the bytes match the ID, so a forwarded case file carries the same evidentiary weight as the original. — _Trust records get cited in arguments ('done? show me the case ID'). If the citation can be quietly edited or can't be checked, it's an anecdote, not a receipt. Rekor's log-index/UUID pattern shows citability is what turns a log into a ledger._
- **TL-3** (v1) — The verdict states its scope of validity in plain words, above the fold: 'This is a statement about svc-a@abc123f + svc-b@d4e5f6a as they ran on 2026-07-17 21:40 UTC. It says nothing about any other version.' The record never claims one inch more than it observed. — _This one sentence is the anti-decay seed. A verdict that admits its scope at write time can be honestly aged later; a bare green checkmark is a lie waiting for the next deploy. This is also where Rust-compiler message craft applies: precise, on your side, never vague._
- **TL-4** (v1) — The record captures the touched-surface manifest: which services, endpoints, queues, and data the skeptical walk actually crossed, each with its fingerprint — and narrates it ('the walk crossed 3 services and 14 requests; here is each one'). — _The walk itself discovers the promise's true dependency slice — observed, not hand-declared. This is simultaneously riveting narration for beat 3 and the exact raw material verdict-decay detection needs later. Skip it in v1 and every old case file is decay-blind forever._
- **TL-5** (v1) — A machine-readable verdict block is embedded inside the case file (promise, verdict, subjects-by-digest, touched-surface manifest, timings), so a future ledger can index ten thousand case files by folding over files — no re-runs, no HTML scraping, no migration. — _The guardrail says no ledger in v1; this is how we obey it without foreclosing it. The ledger becomes a pure function over records that already exist. In-toto's statement model (subject digests + typed predicate) proves this layering works at ecosystem scale._
- **TL-6** (v1) — A re-opened case file never impersonates the present: the verified-at moment and code fingerprints are the permanent headline framing ('verdict about THAT system, THEN'), so an old green can be trusted as history without being mistaken for a claim about today. — _The cheapest way a trust product starts lying is an old PASS being read as a current one. v1 can't compute live staleness (self-contained file, no daemon) — but it can make misreading structurally hard, which is the honest bound._
- **TL-7** (defer) — A queryable ledger over accumulated case files answers the team's real questions from the terminal: 'what is verified right now?', 'show every verdict ever rendered on this promise', 'what changed this week that nothing has verified?' — with the files themselves as the database, no server. — _This is where per-run trust becomes compounding trust (beat 7): 'done' becomes a queryable state of the world instead of a memory. cargo-vet shows plain records in the repo, queried by a CLI, are enough to run real trust programs at Mozilla scale._
- **TL-8** (defer) — Freshness is computed at read time and shown in the ledger's own tri-state, mirroring the verdict valve: STILL BINDING (every touched fingerprint byte-identical today), STALE (names exactly what moved: 'svc-b abc123→9f8e7d; 2 of 14 exercised requests crossed svc-b'), or CAN'T TELL (names the touched surface it cannot fingerprint). Never a bare green on an aged verdict, never a confidence percentage. — _This is the product's answer to verdict decay rendered as an experience: the ledger never asks to be believed — it shows its comparison. Bazel's '(cached) PASSED' label is the craft precedent: memoized trust must be visibly memoized._
- **TL-9** (defer) — Decay is scoped by intersection, not by time: a deploy the walk never touched cannot stale its verdict; a change to anything it did touch stales it immediately; time alone never stales anything. Unfingerprintable mutables the walk touched (third-party APIs, drifting data) yield CAN'T TELL, with the culprit named. — _TTLs are superstition and whole-world invalidation makes the ledger cry wolf until it's ignored. Intersection with the observed touched-surface is the only invalidation rule that is both honest and quiet._
- **TL-10** (defer) — When trust decays, the ledger proposes the smallest repair, ranked by restored coverage: 'one 90-second re-run of the checkout walk would restore 4 of your 6 stale promises.' Re-verification is always targeted and always the user's call — never a background treadmill re-verifying the world. — _This is the economics that makes a trust ledger sustainable: verification spend flows to where trust actually decayed. cargo-vet's 'suggest' (compute the smallest delta audit that restores coverage) is the proven pattern._
- **TL-11** (defer) — Verdicts weave into the team flow: pasting a case ID in a PR or Slack unfurls to promise + verdict + freshness + the broken-moment thumbnail, and a 'done' claim without a case ID starts to read, culturally, as unverified. — _Trust compounds socially or not at all. The ledger wins when 'show me the case file' becomes the team's reflex — the same way 'link the trace' became reflex for teams on Playwright._
- **TL-12** (defer) — Per-promise history: one promise's verdicts across code versions as a timeline — when it broke, what caught it, how long it stayed red, which change restored it. — _The longitudinal view is what turns verdicts into institutional memory ('checkout has broken 3 times this quarter, always in svc-b') and is the first artifact a new teammate can actually learn the system's fragility from._
- **TL-13** (defer) — The ledger itself is tamper-evident and auditable: entries are append-only, deletions and edits are detectable by anyone holding the ledger, and a team can hand the whole record to an outside auditor without asking them to trust the team. — _A trust record that its operator can quietly rewrite is worth nothing in the one moment it matters — a dispute. Rekor/Certificate Transparency established the bar: the log earns trust by being independently verifiable, not by being operated by someone nice._
- **TL-14** (defer) — A verdict can be exported as a portable, signed attestation (promise + subject digests + verdict) consumable outside the team — by a policy gate, a compliance archive, or a customer — without access to the originating system. — _The endgame of compounding trust is that pb's verdicts become currency beyond the team that minted them; in-toto's envelope/statement layering shows how to do portability without inventing a format._

**Learn from (quality bar):**

- **Sigstore Rekor (transparency log)** (https://github.com/sigstore/rekor) — A record of trust must not require trusting its keeper. Rekor earns adoption by making the ledger itself verifiable: every entry gets a durable, citable identity (log index / UUID) people paste into conversations; inclusion proofs can be 'stapled' to an artifact so the claim stays checkable offline, next to the thing it describes; and rekor-monitor lets anyone audit that the log stayed append-only. Steal all three moves: citable case IDs, proof traveling WITH the artifact (the self-verifying case file), and tamper-evidence checkable by outsiders — because trust records exist for disputes, and in a dispute 'trust our database' is worth zero.
- **Bazel (test-result caching + hermeticity)** (https://bazel.build/basics/hermeticity) — Bazel is the deepest prior art on 'when is yesterday's PASS still valid?' — its answer is validity keyed to a digest of the complete input closure, and its craft is HONEST LABELING: a memoized result always renders as '(cached) PASSED', never as a fresh run. Its failure modes are the vertical's two design walls: an input that escapes the hash produces a falsely-green cache hit (the unforgivable lie), while a volatile input in the hash (workspace status) over-invalidates until the cache is useless (the cry-wolf trap). Steal the display rule — an aged verdict is never rendered as a current one — and the asymmetry: when forced to choose, over-invalidate, because stale-marked truth is annoying but a false green is fatal.
- **cargo-vet (Mozilla)** (https://mozilla.github.io/cargo-vet/) — cargo-vet is verdict decay solved as an economics problem: audits are recorded as plain files scoped to exact versions ('safe-to-deploy for 1.2'), moving to 1.3 automatically voids nothing and asserts nothing — coverage is simply recomputed — and 'cargo vet suggest' computes the SMALLEST delta audit that restores full coverage. Steal three things: claims scoped to exact fingerprints so aging is computable rather than argued about; recomputing coverage at read time instead of mutating old records; and answering decay with a ranked minimal-repair plan ('one 90s re-run restores 4 promises') instead of a re-verify-everything treadmill. Also note its adoption craft: the trust records live in the repo and are diffed and code-reviewed like code — trust changes get the same scrutiny as source changes.

**Open questions:** Fingerprint granularity vs noise: at whole-service digest granularity, a busy repo stales most verdicts weekly — is that acceptable cry-wolf load for v2, and what evidence bar must finer change→route slicing meet before it may claim STILL BINDING across an unrelated commit to a touched service?; Data decay: the walk exercised specific data states — is schema-hash plus sampled invariants an honest-enough data fingerprint to ever say STILL BINDING, or does any mutable datastore the walk touched permanently force CAN'T TELL?; Promise identity over time: what makes two promises 'the same' so history threads correctly — hash of the sentence (breaks on rewording), a user-named slug (config surface the guardrail resists), or fuzzy matching (can silently lie)?; Where does a TEAM's ledger live without violating the no-server/no-auth ethos — case files committed to the repo, a shared object-store folder, or both — and what is the concurrency/merge story when two runs finish at once?; Does STALE ever degrade to CAN'T TELL — after N intervening changes the 'what moved' diff is so long it stops being information — and if so, is N principled or arbitrary?; Citability strength: is the content-derived, self-verifying case ID enough for teams to treat case files as evidence in disputes, or does real-world citation require actual signing (sigstore-style) — and can that be added later without re-minting old records?; Freshness needs a 'now' to compare against: must the live system be reachable to compute freshness, or should the ledger also compare against a declared target (deploy manifest / lockfile) so freshness works when the system is down — and are those two answers labeled differently?; Ledger scale-out: when case files number in the tens of thousands, does 'files as the database' still answer 'what is verified right now?' in interactive time, or does an index cache appear — and how does that cache stay honest (rebuildable from files, never authoritative)?
